Purpose

Primary purpose

Design, launch and run Alpha Hours: a premium after-school program that delivers the Alpha day, mastery-based academics on Timeback, followed by a life skills workshop, to families who do not yet attend Alpha and current Alpha students whose school day ends at 3:30. Families attend four hours a week: a two-night track (Monday/Wednesday or Tuesday/Thursday, 4:00 to 6:00 pm) or a single four-hour Saturday block (two are offered), all inside existing Alpha campuses. Every block runs Timeback in pomodoros, 25-minute mastery sessions, ends with life skills and shoutouts, and families enroll one seven-week session at a time, five sessions a year.

The program exists to do four things at once

  • Generate profit from a premium after-school offering priced above every competitor.
  • Use real estate we already pay for, turning empty late-afternoon, evening and weekend hours in Alpha buildings into revenue.
  • Feed Alpha. Give families a low-friction, paid way to try the Alpha model, and convert them into full-time enrollment. Conversion is the one modeled number in this plan, and Phase 1 exists to replace it with a measured one.
  • Serve Alpha's own families. Almost every Alpha campus ends the school day at 3:30 with nothing behind it. Alpha Hours closes that gap on every campus, and gets paid a premium price to do it. Current Alpha students enroll at the same session price with a 5 percent family discount, and transition directly on campus at the end of the school day.

Success is measured on all four: site profit, building utilization, conversions to full Alpha, and current-family enrollment. Underneath them sit Alpha's three commitments, and every one carries into Alpha Hours with its own measurement.

Kids love school. Measured by session-to-session re-enrollment and attendance, in addition to student surveys, and it is the leading indicator for everything else. A child who begs to come back is what fills the cohorts, climbs the mastery data, and converts the family. Profit and conversion are the outcomes, love of the program is the engine, and we track it accordingly.

Learn 2x. Students learn at the accelerated pace Alpha holds itself to during their academic minutes, measured by following every student's academic progress and learning velocity in Timeback, session over session. We do not promise 2x learning in one session, but promise the roadmap to 2x learning should the student enroll full-time at an Alpha School.

Learn life skills. Every session culminates in an AlphaTest Showcase, where students are tested on the life skills they have been working on, in front of their parents. The students will already be convinced by the time they have the showcase; it exists as a mechanism for convincing the parents.

Out of scope

  • Custom changes to Timeback for after-school use. The program runs the platform as built, not subject-by-subject.
  • Certified-teacher tutoring models or one-on-one private tutoring. Alpha Hours cannot accommodate students who need 1:1 support, and says so publicly on the landing page.
  • Test-prep-only offerings (SAT and ACT, APs).
  • Non-academic enrichment (arts, sports, social clubs) as the core product.
  • Full-day or full-time schooling. This is strictly after-school and Saturday.
  • Online-only delivery. The program is in-person inside Alpha buildings.
  • Childcare or custodial care. Alpha Hours is academic instruction, structured to qualify as instruction under state exemptions.
  • Subjects beyond Timeback. Grades outside K-8 at launch; a dedicated middle and high school evening block launches Fall 2027.

Spiky Points of View

SPOV 1: It's bad business to give away all of our trials for free when we can make money on them.

Traditional school admissions gives free trials to children because the schools need the families more than the families need the school. Alpha Schools do this today, too, with free shadow days, staff time and resources given away. Alpha Hours inverts this: it funnels a portion of prospective families into a paid experience, monetizing the trial that otherwise would have been free. There's an opportunity to make money here that we shouldn't throw away.

SPOV 2: Every pathway to yes outside of Alpha is losing us money.

At Alpha a no is never a no forever. For every student who is not admitted, it is a "not yet" with a pathway to yes. Currently, the pathway to yes is feedback and roadmap. We can turn it into a monetized pathway to yes inside of Alpha: students who are a "not now" for Alpha Schools can join Alpha Hours with clear feedback and a roadway that they pay for, and where we can more thoroughly evaluate the child and his or her progress.

SPOV 3: A pathway to yes with no way to measure it or keep the families in our funnel is worthless.

We can make our "pathway to yes" concrete and measurable, generating future revenue. Our current pathway to yes is feedback and a roadmap. We can take this and make it concrete and measurable, while gathering more detailed data over time on each student.

SPOV 4: One shadow day is inferior to seven weeks of data.

Some students are stand out "yeses" on a shadow day, but many are not. The data we gather in the course of a few hours is inferior to data gathered over seven weeks. We can boost the quality of our student population by using Alpha Hours to gather more detailed data on students who are not stand out "yeses."

SPOV 5: Alpha Hours is a stronger qualifier than $100 and some unmeasured level of interest.

Currently, families apply with a $100 application fee, and are handed a free Alpha experience. $100 does not indicate ability or willingness to pay $40,000-75,000 per year. Alpha Hours would be our most powerful qualifier: the families are willing to invest $4,500 and four hours a week for seven weeks. This tells us that a family has a strong level of interest, and is willing to pay.

SPOV 6: 100 minutes a week and a roadmap is superior to six hours a day and a letter grade.

100 weekly minutes of Timeback produces something no school or human teacher in America can give a parent: a precise mastery map of where the child actually is in every subject, the child's measured learning velocity, and a computed answer to the only question that matters, which is exactly how many weeks at full Alpha until this child is at grade level, or the next one. The traditional school has the child six hours a day for years and produces a report card guessing subjectively at all of this. Seven weeks with us produces the map and the timeline. We do not compete with Kumon. We compete with six hours of no information, and we win in seven weeks.

SPOV 7: Every building sitting empty after 3:30 PM is a waste of assets that we should be sweating.

We have buildings, owned or leased, across the country that are sitting empty for the majority of hours in the week. We should be sweating our assets to maximum capacity. Alpha Hours is just the start of a path to making money on every single available hour.

Insights

Insight 1: Alpha Hours turns admissions from a cost center into a P&L. Once trials are paid and the pathway to yes is monetized, admissions stops being pure spend: shadow-day staff time becomes billable program hours and the marketing dollars behind every inquiry are recovered inside the funnel itself. This only happens if routing is policy, not improvisation. Admissions needs a formal disposition step for every family: yes, not yet with an Alpha Hours roadmap, or no. The moment routing is left to individual judgment, the revenue leaks back to free shadow days.

Insight 2: The "not yet" path to yes letter becomes a product. Today a not yet costs goodwill and loses the family to another school's funnel. A not yet that ships with an Alpha Hours seat, a concrete roadmap of mastery targets, and a scheduled re-evaluation date converts a rejection into revenue, retention, and a second look on better data. The roadmap must name measurable thresholds, so re-application becomes a data check instead of a second subjective judgment.

Insight 3: Seven weeks of data should produce the admissions decision, so set the bar before the session starts. Before each session opens, we define exactly what a child needs to show to earn an invitation to apply: how much they mastered, how consistently they showed up and completed their sessions. Then the decision makes itself. By the end of seven weeks, the data says yes or not yet, the same way the week-1 exit process works: the standard is set in advance and the record makes the call, not a gut feeling. It means we can raise the quality of the students we admit without admitting fewer of them.

Insight 4: Alpha Hours families are the most qualified families in the funnel, so measure them differently. A family that pays $4,500 and commits four hours a week for seven weeks has told us far more than a family that pays $100 and fills out a form. Alpha Hours does not replace the existing funnel, it adds a layer of deeply qualified families on top of it. The measures that capture it: qualified families enrolled in the program, conversion to full Alpha by number of sessions completed, and cost per enrollment, which turns negative because the family pays us while they decide.

Insight 5: The data builds over time, and it moves with the child. A shadow day gives us a few hours of impressions, and they fade. Alpha Hours gives us weeks of real data on what the child has mastered and the life skills they learn, and the data grows every session. When that family enrolls at full Alpha, the data comes with them: the child starts on day one at exactly the right level, no testing, no ramp-up. The same data that tells us whether to admit the child also tells us where to start them. No other school's trial produces either.

Insight 6: Sweating the assets has an order of operations. Alpha Hours fills the first slice, weekday afternoons and Saturdays, and in doing so puts a trained team, insurance, and an operating playbook in place at every campus, all carried by that first program. Once that foundation exists, every other empty block, early mornings, school-holiday weeks, summer gaps, adult evenings, can be added without rebuilding it, so each new program starts at a higher margin than the one before it. Alpha Hours is not the whole building-utilization strategy. It is just the first block of it.

Insight 7: A monetized pathway to yes must never pay more than the yes. Alpha Hours earns two ways: session fees of about $22,500 a year, and conversions to full Alpha worth $40,000 to $75,000 a year. The session fee is the one the program books on its own P&L, so whoever runs Alpha Hours has a built-in temptation to keep families enrolled as "not yet." Three structures prevent it: the review thresholds that trigger the conversion conversation are published in advance; every family gets a scheduled re-evaluation each session; and any family continuing past five sessions must have an Alpha application on file and pays the significantly higher continuation rate, so a long-stayer is either pipeline or premium revenue, never a parked seat. The last piece is accountability: the Head is reviewed on conversion rate and full-time enrollments, not on fill rate or session revenue.

Knowledge Tree

Category 1: How non-school learning environments drive academic gains

Mastery pedagogy, standardized pacing, and visible progress let after-school programs outperform classrooms, using process-driven personalization rather than teacher variation.

  • Kumon: Founded 1954. Worksheet-based mastery, non-certified staff, 25,000+ centers, $150 to $220 per subject per month. Personalization lives in the process, not the content.
  • Mathnasium: Founded 2002. Trained non-teacher instructors, fixed method, math only, $250 to $500 per month.
  • Fusion Academy: One-to-one private school, $8,000 to $17,000 per course per year. Proves premium willingness to pay for personalized academics.
  • Alpha / Timeback / Alpha Hours: Uses AI to deliver personalized academics equivalent or superior to 1:1 tutoring with a human, keeping each child in his or her zone of proximal development in each subject, delivering 2x learning (Alpha's internal data).

Insights: Mastery scales when the system, not the teacher, carries instruction. Parents validate trust through visible, measurable progress. Premium after-school is about quality of result, not quantity of hours. Alpha Hours will provide personalized academic reports at the start and end of every session.

Category 2: After-school business models and the feeder

After-school programs scale through standardization, real-estate leverage, and, for a school-owned program, conversion to full enrollment.

  • Market size: US families already spend about $40B a year on tutoring and after-school combined: tutoring $18.9B, growing ~11%/yr (IBISWorld 2025); after-school $21.0B with 22.6M children unable to get a spot (Afterschool Alliance 2025). The full 40-campus network at capacity is $267M, well under 1 percent of what families already spend today. The constraint is buildings, not demand.
  • Demand evidence, bottom-up: In the last 14 days, 3 of 23 NYC admissions conversations included an unprompted ask for after-school, 13 percent, with no marketing and no product to point to. Alpha parents nationwide ask what happens after 3:30, and today the answer is nothing. Price acceptance is proven at this exact number: Alpha Summer in the Hamptons has 174 paid registrations at $4,500 per week, with one price objection total.
  • Huntington: Founded 1977. Diagnostic-driven, multi-subject, $40 to $75 per hour. Assessment builds trust and justifies price.
  • Premium pricing precedent: Command Education $120,000 a year, up to $750,000 per multi-year package; Lindamood-Bell $10,000+ for six weeks; Fusion $60,000+ full-time. All price against outcome value, not hours, and all quote the package, not the hour.
  • Feeder economics: A private school typically spends $3,700 to $5,800 in marketing to win one enrolled student. Alpha Hours flips that: the prospective family pays us $4,500 a session while they decide. Every session ends with a conversion artifact: the child's mastery map and a personalized projection of weeks-to-grade-level at full Alpha. We model conversion at 8 to 12 percent, and Phase 1 exists to replace that assumption with a measured number. Each conversion is worth $160,000 to $325,000 in lifetime tuition, which is why the feeder pipeline ($230M to $340M nationally) is worth more than the program's own profit.

Insights: Standardization plus assessment builds scalable trust. Real estate already controlled is the margin. For a school-owned program, the feeder is the largest source of value, and it only functions with an intentional, structured handoff.

Business Model and Operations

The program

An Alpha day runs primarily on two things: mastery-based academics on Timeback and life skills workshops. Alpha Hours compresses that whole day into one or two blocks. A weekday block is two hours, and requires a Monday/Wednesday or Tuesday/Thursday registration: a ten-minute Limitless launch, two 25-minute academic sessions on Timeback with a short break between, a 40-minute life skills workshop, and ten minutes of shoutouts. Each of the two Saturday blocks runs the same day at double length: four 25-minute academic sessions, an 80-minute workshop, and a snack break in the middle. Either way the child gets the same week: 100 minutes of focused, adaptive mastery academics and 80 minutes of life skills. Timeback delivers the instruction and adapts to each child. The guide holds focus, effort, and motivation, and leads the workshop.

Motivation and incentives

The child has already been through six hours of regular school, walks in tired at 4 pm, and needs a reason to give full effort. Alpha has this reason built into the model twice. First, Timeback's own promise: get through your academics with focus, and the rest of the block is an amazing life skills workshop, the part of the block built around things kids actually love. Second, the extrinsic motivational model: students earn the Alpha currency for XP earned in Timeback, and can purchase items in the Alpha Hours Emporium. Both keep the child working diligently and boost love of Alpha Hours.

AlphaTest Showcase

Every session ends with an AlphaTest Showcase in the final week. Students are tested on the life skills they have worked on across the session's workshops, and parents come to see it. It is how the life skills commitment gets measured instead of assumed, and it puts every parent in the building in the same week they receive their child's mastery map and weeks-to-grade-level report, which makes it the natural setting for the re-enrollment and full-Alpha conversation.

Schedule and tracks

Runs Monday through Thursday afternoons and Saturday, inside existing Alpha campuses. The weekday block starts right where the Alpha school day ends, so the building never goes dark and every child is home for dinner.

Weekday: guide setup 3:30 to 3:45 pm; drop-off 3:45 to 4:00 pm (current Alpha students transition directly on campus); block 4:00 to 6:00 pm; dismissal and clean up 6:00 to 6:15 pm. Saturday: setup 8:45 to 9:00 am; AM block 9:00 am to 1:00 pm; transition 1:00 to 2:00 pm (staggered pickup and drop-off, no lunch served); PM block 2:00 to 6:00 pm; clean up 6:00 to 6:15 pm.

Every campus runs four cohorts a week: Monday/Wednesday, Tuesday/Thursday, Saturday morning, and Saturday afternoon. The same room and the same guide team serve all four, and every cohort gets the same four weekly hours.

Middle and high school block (launches Fall 2027): weekdays 6:15 to 8:15 pm, Monday/Wednesday or Tuesday/Thursday. Same spine as the K-8 block, with Alpha Max and Alpha X programming filling the workshop slot. The block adds two cohorts per campus and lifts the enrolled ceiling from four times room capacity to six.

Capacity and enrollment

Enrolled students per campus is four times the room capacity: two weekday tracks plus two Saturday blocks. A 100-seat room fills to 400 enrolled students; from Fall 2027 the middle and high school block lifts the ceiling to six times. Five sessions a year, each about seven weeks, one session at a time with a one-session minimum. Current Alpha students enroll at the same session price with a 5 percent family discount. Continuing past five sessions requires a completed Alpha application on file and the continuation rate: $6,500 per session in premium markets, $5,000 standard. Where a cohort holds a waitlist, families with applications on file hold their seats; current Alpha students are exempt.

Hired for experience with children and trained out of teaching; the core discipline of the role is restraint.
  • Staffing: about 1 guide per 10 students, two-adult minimum, extra attention to the youngest. The same team serves all four cohorts.

Compliance

Design and market as academic instruction, not childcare, to qualify for state exemptions. Avoid meals where they trigger licensing (Florida especially). Sign a formal host-campus facility-use agreement. Get a written exemption determination per state before opening; flag the Saturday format specifically. FBI background checks on every hire; minimum two adults on site at all times. Insurance: general liability, professional liability, workers' compensation, and sexual abuse and molestation coverage.

Pricing

Per session, priced on value not hours. Standard markets $3,500 per session ($17,500 per year); premium markets $4,500 ($22,500 per year). Current Alpha families: 5 percent discount ($4,275 premium, $3,325 standard). Continuation rate past five sessions: $6,500 premium, $5,000 standard, Alpha application required. The pilot prices every campus at the premium tier, Chicago included. A session is about 28 hours; at $4,500 that is roughly $161 an hour, just under elite 1:1 tutors who cover one subject; we cover multiple, supervised, with the data to prove progress. The full year is roughly a third of full Alpha tuition. The price point is market-tested: 174 paid Alpha Summer registrations at $4,500 per week, one price objection total.

Downside economics

Contribution per enrolled student is about $20,625 a year. Minimum operating cost per campus is about $278K, so break-even is 14 enrolled students per campus, about 4 per cohort. Enrollment closes before each session starts and guides are hired to sold seats. Two mechanical floors: a cohort that has not sold 10 seats two weeks before session start does not open, and a campus below break-even at week 3 of Session 2 closes at session end. The maximum downside at any site is seven weeks of payroll.

Potential Objections and Challenges

Reputation. Alpha Hours will be many families' first Alpha experience. This is why NYC, the network's most valuable pipeline and most visible market, is held out of the pilot entirely and opens Fall 2027 with a proven playbook. Quality is carried by the guide training program, staffing guides to sold seats, the Lead Guide at every site, and the AlphaTest Showcase every seven weeks.

Wrong-fit students, and the week 1 exit process. The exit trigger is behavioral and mechanical, not a judgment call: every incident that pulls a guide off the room is logged the moment it happens, and two logged incidents in week 1 is an exit before week 2, with the incident record and a prorated refund. A child who requires a level of individual support that compromises the three commitments for the rest of the room is the second trigger. Both standards are published at enrollment; the log makes the decision, the Head delivers it.

Students who need 1:1 support. The staffing model is 1 guide per 10 students and cannot deliver one-to-one support. This is publicly listed on the landing page before any family pays.

The arrival window. Outside schools dismiss around 3:00 and drop-off closes at 4:00. For most target families this is routine, and for the rest the two Saturday blocks carry the load. The window is held at 15 minutes on purpose: the block starts on time for everyone, every time.

Saturday competes with youth sports. Two Saturday blocks, two weekday tracks, and session-at-a-time enrollment let families rotate around sports seasons instead of choosing against them permanently.

The parking risk. Any family past five sessions holds an Alpha application and pays the continuation rate, $6,500 premium and $5,000 standard, so a long-stayer is either pipeline by definition or premium revenue, and where a waitlist stands, non-applicants release the seat first.

Guide supply at national scale. The candidate pool, public school teachers and retired teachers, exists at scale in every metro, and the Alpha Hours training program standardizes quality before any guide runs a room.

Rollout

Phase 1 (Session 2, Fall 2026): three-market pilot, run as a conversion experiment. Chicago, Boston (two sites), Greenwich (Armonk). Every campus opens at full capacity, all four cohorts on sale. Chicago opens on its current 94-seat footprint ahead of the expansion. NYC is deliberately not in the pilot; New York gets Alpha Hours in Fall 2027, with a proven playbook. The pilot succeeds on two measured numbers: conversion to full Alpha at or above 8 percent and session-over-session re-enrollment at or above 70 percent, tracked through a six-stage funnel, with mechanical triggers published in advance, measured as lift against a matched baseline, and a pre-committed decision rule: below 6 percent pause, 6 to 10 percent measured regional pace, above 10 percent full national go.

Phase 2 (Session 3, January 2027): the Chicago expansion. From 94 seats to 517, lifting the pilot from 1,256 to 2,948 enrolled. The national build in miniature: the same room, the same playbook, five and a half times the seats, executed mid-pilot.

Phase 3 (Session 4, Spring 2027): national launch. The playbook rolls across the country with two full sessions of operating data and the first measured conversion numbers.

Phase 4 (Fall 2027): New York, and the middle and high school block. Alpha Hours opens at Alpha New York at 125 seats, 500 enrolled at capacity. The same season, the 6:15 to 8:15 pm block launches, lifting every participating campus ceiling by 50 percent.

Why these campuses. Chicago is the network's largest campus and tests the model at the exact scale the national build depends on, in a market outside the East Coast corridor, which makes the measured conversion number generalize. Greenwich and Boston are dense UHNW markets at premium tuition where every conversion is worth the most, close enough that one launch team and a shared guide pool cover three campuses. NYC is excluded on purpose: the admissions pipeline there is already strong and the brand risk is highest, so New York opens Fall 2027, after the model is proven.

Alpha's learning-speed and test-score figures are Alpha's own internal data and are not independently audited.
