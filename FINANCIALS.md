# Alpha Hours: Financials

## Owner

Nancy Wisniewski Torvund

## Summary

Alpha Hours launches October 19, 2026 as a mastery learning center inside six Alpha campuses: New York (180 Maiden Lane, 100 seats), Austin (Spyglass, 100), Miami (Tropical Park, 60), Chicago (Lakeshore East, 60), Greenwich (40) and Boston (Beacon Hill, 40). Membership is monthly: $1,750 in premium markets, $1,250 in standard markets (Austin), for two guided one-hour visits a week plus daily Timeback at home. Every seat serves ten members. Every figure in this document comes from one model, the same one behind the BrainLift, and the assumptions Year 1 exists to replace are listed at the end.

## Assumptions

Price. Base tier (two visits a week) $1,750 a month premium, $1,250 standard. Plus tier (three visits) $2,250 / $1,600. Daily tier $3,000 / $2,150. Enrollment fee $750. Current Alpha families 5 percent off. No sibling discount. Annual prepay at list. Waitlist repricing: two consecutive months with a waitlist reprices new memberships by $100 a month. Annual step: $100 a month every September for new members. All figures below are at the base tier and year-one list price; the higher tiers, repricing and the current-family discount are not modeled and net out positive.

Capacity. Twenty visit slots a week per seat (Monday to Thursday at 3:30, 4:30, 5:30 and 6:30; Saturday at 9:00, 10:00, 11:00 and 12:00). Two visits per member. Members per seat: ten. A 20-seat room: 200 members.

Calendar. Year 1 is twelve billing months from October 19, 2026 to October 18, 2027. Membership runs through the summer. Fill is the share of member capacity and ramps monthly.

Cost model. Variable cost per member per year, premium $1,820: Timeback $750, rewards and Emporium $200, MAP tests $40, referral marketing $200, payment processing 3 percent of fees ($630). Standard $1,640. Floor guides: one per 20 seats, part-time at $30 an hour for 22 hours a week, 48 weeks, $31,680 a year, scheduled to fill with a floor of one quarter of the seat schedule. Per-campus fixed cost $213,000: Lead Guide $100,000, Center Coordinator $60,000, facility $45,000, insurance $8,000. Central team $1.35M: Head $400,000, Operations VP $200,000, Admissions Integration Lead $150,000, AI-first engineer $200,000, Finance and compliance $150,000, tooling $250,000. Charged in full against Year 1.

Churn. Four percent a month in the enrollment-fee calculation, replaced by the measured number.

## Cash timing

The enrollment fee lands at sign-up and each month is billed in advance on the enrollment anniversary, so revenue is collected before cost. Floor guides are paid by the slot, Timeback is billed monthly, fixed cost runs monthly. In the plan case month one collects $950K against about $250K of cost. The program never needs working capital from the school, and every campus is cash-positive from its first month at anything above eleven members.

## Downside, floors and sensitivities

Contribution per premium member is $19,180 a year. Fixed cost per campus is $213,000, so break-even is 11 members. Floor guides are scheduled to fill, so site margin holds above 85 percent at any fill above break-even, and the maximum downside at any site is one month of fixed cost: no capex, no lease, part-time floor staff, month-to-month membership.

Two mechanical floors. Slot floor: a slot with fewer than five members after the third month closes and its members move to an adjacent slot. Site floor: a campus below 11 members at the month-3 checkpoint (January 18, 2027) drops to Saturday only until it clears 11.

### Satellites, and why not franchises

A company-owned satellite is a Kumon-sized storefront (about 1,500 square feet, 15 to 20 seats) in a metro where a campus center has held 70 percent fill for two months. At 150 members and premium pricing: $3.15M revenue, about $0.64M cost (variable $0.27M, rent $0.12M, the campus fixed team $0.21M, floor guides $0.04M), about $2.5M profit.

The same center franchised at a Kumon-style royalty of 23 percent of tuition: Alpha collects $0.72M in royalty, pays $0.11M of Timeback cost, nets about $0.6M; the franchisee keeps about $1.9M. Franchising gives away four fifths of the profit and all of the data and control. It is evaluated once, in Year 3, only for standard-tier markets Alpha will not enter with a campus or a satellite, and only at a royalty above 40 percent.

## The feeder

One conversion to full Alpha is worth $160,000 to $325,000 in lifetime tuition, $240,000 at the midpoint. Modeled conversion is 6 to 14 percent of the Considering and Path to yes lanes, replaced by the measured number after Year 1. The program clears its P&L at zero conversions, so acquisition cost per full enrollment is negative: the family pays $1,750 a month while it decides, against a private-school industry median of about $3,700 in marketing spend per enrolled student.

| Base | 6 percent | 10 percent | 14 percent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plan peak, 800 families in two lanes | 48 enrollments, $11.5M | 80, $19.2M | 112, $26.9M |
| Pilot capacity, 1,600 families in two lanes | 96, $23M | 160, $38M | 224, $54M |

## Market size

TAM about $40B a year: what US families already spend on tutoring ($18.9B) and after-school ($21.0B). SAM about $16B: that spend in Alpha's metros, roughly 40 percent of premium households. SOM $461M: the full network at capacity, under 3 percent of SAM. The constraint is fill and Lead Guides, not demand.

## What Year 1 replaces

In order of how much they move the numbers. Each one is measured and reported by campus, slot and lane at the month-3 checkpoint (January 18, 2027) and every month after.

1. Fill and its ramp. The plan says 50 percent of member capacity by month twelve.
2. Monthly retention. The plan needs 92 percent; the Stay lane's value rests on it.
3. Home-practice compliance. The dose depends on the days between visits.
4. Lane mix. Modeled at 55 / 25 / 15 / 5. Revenue does not change with it; the feeder and tenure do.
5. Price ceiling. Waitlist depth at $1,750 says how far the repricing rule goes.
6. Conversion. Modeled at 6 to 14 percent of two lanes. Upside only.
7. Floor-guide ratio. One per 20 seats at $30 an hour.
