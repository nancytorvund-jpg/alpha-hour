# Alpha Hours: Financials

## Owner

Nancy Wisniewski Torvund

## Summary

Alpha Hours launches in Session 2, October 19 to December 18, 2026, at six campuses in four markets: New York (180 Maiden Lane, 100 seats; 156 William Street, 30 seats), Greenwich (Armonk, 80), Boston (40 and 100), Chicago (94, stepping to 517 in January 2027). Every campus is priced at $4,500 per seven-week session, five sessions a year. The plan is sized on families who will never enroll at Alpha: seven in ten seats on the modeled mix, and the most durable seven in ten, because nothing about their reason for not enrolling changes year to year. Conversions to full Alpha are upside and nothing in the plan depends on them. Every figure in this document comes from one model, the same one behind the BrainLift, and the assumptions that Session 2 exists to replace are listed at the end.

## Assumptions

Price. $4,500 per session in premium markets, $3,500 standard. Every pilot campus is premium. Current Alpha families pay 5 percent less ($4,275). From the sixth session a seat reprices to $5,000 ($4,000 standard) unless the family has an Alpha application on file. A cohort that closes a session with a waitlist reprices by $500 the next session. Break weeks are $4,500 a week. All figures below are at the year-one list price; the continuation rate, waitlist repricing, and the current-family discount are not modeled and net out to a small positive.

Capacity. Room capacity is seats filled at one time. Four cohorts share every room (Monday/Wednesday, Tuesday/Thursday, Saturday morning, Saturday afternoon), so enrolled = seats x 4. The middle and high school evening block adds two cohorts, enrolled = seats x 6, where it runs.

Calendar. Five seven-week sessions a year. Session 2: October 19 to December 18, 2026. Session 3: January 4 to February 19, 2027. Session 4: February 24 to April 16, 2027. Session 5: April 26 to June 18, 2027. Year 1 is Sessions 2 through 5, four sessions. Break weeks: April 19 to 23, 2027 in Year 1, two a year from Year 2.

Cost model. Per student per session $435 ($2,175 a year): Timeback $150, the extrinsic motivation model (Alpha bucks redeemed in the Emporium, inventory at cost) $40, supplies $15, snacks $30, the child's share of the AlphaTest Showcase $5, payment processing 3 percent ($135), local marketing $60. Guides: one per 10 seats, part-time at $50,000, hired to sold seats, the same guide serving all four cohorts, so a campus carries seats divided by 10 guides. Per-campus fixed cost $188,000: Lead Guide $75,000, Campus Coordinator $50,000 (a 50 percent role), facility $45,000, insurance $8,000, AlphaTest Showcase events $10,000 ($2,000 per campus per session for four cohort showcases). William Street shares New York's coordinator, so its fixed cost is $138,000. Central team $700,000 base (Head $400,000, Operations and Admin VP $200,000, $100,000 operations support), about $875,000 fully loaded, charged in full against Year 1.

Devices. The building's own laptops are free after 3:30. A loaner pool covering 20 percent of seats is in the launch budget for campuses that run short.

## Revenue by lane

Revenue per seat is the same in every lane. The lane changes tenure and what the family is worth beyond the session fee. The plan is sized on the Stay lane, families who will never enroll at Alpha; the other two prospective lanes are upside. The mix is a modeled assumption Session 2 replaces.

| Lane | Share | Families at expanded capacity | Session revenue a year | Value beyond the session fee |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stay (never enrolling, the business) | 70% | 2,428 | $54.6M | Three years at year-one then continuation rates: $72,500 a family, before break weeks; five years $122,500 |
| Considering (upside) | 15% | 520 | $11.7M | Two sessions ($9,000) plus 20 percent conversion at $240,000 lifetime tuition: $57,000 expected, non-converters move to Stay |
| Path to yes (upside) | 5% | 173 | $3.9M | Three sessions ($13,500) plus a modeled 20 percent admit rate at $240,000: $61,500 expected |
| Current Alpha families | 10% | 347 | $7.4M (after 5 percent discount) | Retention of the family already paying full tuition |

Stay tenure is the number that compounds. At 70 percent re-enrollment session over session, the average Stay family stays about 3.3 sessions; at 85 percent, about 6.7. Every ten points of re-enrollment on the Stay lane is worth more than doubling the conversion rate.

## Cash timing

Enrollment closes before each session starts. The $500 deposit lands at sign-up and the balance is due before day one, so each session's revenue is collected before its costs are paid. Guides are paid across the seven weeks, Timeback is billed per session, fixed cost runs monthly. In the plan case Session 2 collects $4.8M before October 19 against about $0.9M of cost paid out through December 18. The program never needs working capital from the school, and every campus is cash-positive from its first session at anything above break-even.

## Downside, floors, and sensitivities

Contribution per enrolled student is $20,325 a year before guides. Minimum operating cost per campus (fixed plus the two-adult minimum) is about $288K, so break-even is 15 enrolled students per campus, about 4 per cohort, under 4 percent of full capacity at the largest sites. Guides are hired to sold seats, so site margin holds above 79 percent at every fill level above break-even, and the maximum downside at any site is seven weeks of payroll: no capex, no lease, part-time staff, one-session enrollment.

Two mechanical floors. Cohort floor: a cohort that has not sold 10 seats two weeks before session start (October 5, 2026 for Session 2) does not open, and its families roll into another cohort. Site floor: a campus below break-even at week 3 of Session 2 (the week of November 2) closes at session end.

## The feeder (upside)

One conversion to full Alpha is worth $160,000 to $325,000 in lifetime tuition, $240,000 at the midpoint. Modeled conversion is 4 to 8 percent of all enrolled families, most of it from the Considering and Path to yes lanes, replaced by the measured number after Session 2. Nothing in the plan above depends on it. The program clears its P&L at zero conversions, so the acquisition cost per full enrollment is negative: the family pays $4,500 a session while it decides, against a private-school industry median of $3,677 in marketing spend per enrolled student.

## Market size

TAM about $40B a year: what US families already spend on tutoring ($18.9B) and after-school ($21.0B), before growth. SAM about $16B: that spend in Alpha's metros, roughly 40 percent of premium households. SOM $268M: the full network at capacity on K-8 blocks, 1.7 percent of SAM. The constraint is buildings, not demand.

## What Session 2 replaces

These are the modeled assumptions, in order of how much they move the numbers. Each one is measured in Session 2 and reported by campus, cohort, lane, and market at the week-3 checkpoint and at session end.

1. Fill. The plan says 60 percent at launch. Every dollar in this document scales with it.
2. Re-enrollment. The plan needs 70 percent session over session; the Stay lane, and so the business, rests on it.
3. Lane mix. Modeled at 70 / 15 / 5 / 10. The revenue does not change with it; tenure and the upside do.
4. Price ceiling. Waitlist depth at $4,500 says how far the repricing rule can go.
5. Conversion. Modeled at 4 to 8 percent of all enrolled. Upside only; nothing in the P&L depends on it.
6. Guide ratio and cost. One per 10 seats at $50,000 part-time. The training program and the first payroll confirm it.
7. The per-child lines. Motivation model $40, snacks $30, showcase $5 plus $2,000 a campus per session. Small, and measured against re-enrollment, because they are the lines that make children want to come back.
