# Alpha Hours

A premium after-school program that runs the Alpha day, mastery-based academics on Timeback followed by a life skills workshop, inside existing Alpha School buildings, for families who do not yet attend Alpha and for current Alpha students whose school day ends at 3:30. It is designed to make money, use real estate Alpha already pays for, feed full-time Alpha enrollment, and serve Alpha's own families.

**Owner:** Nancy Wisniewski Torvund, proposed Head of Alpha Hours.

## The model in one paragraph

The Alpha day, after school: mastery academics on Timeback plus a life skills workshop. Each family picks a two-day track, Monday/Wednesday or Tuesday/Thursday from 4:00 to 6:00 pm, or a single four-hour Saturday block, morning or afternoon; every track gets 100 minutes of academics and 80 minutes of life skills a week. Four cohorts share every room, so a campus enrolls four times its seat count (six times from Fall 2027, when the middle and high school evening block launches). Families enroll one seven-week session at a time; there are five sessions a year, each ending in an AlphaTest Showcase. Priced above every competitor at $4,500 per session in premium markets, about a third of full Alpha tuition.

## Headline numbers

- **Three-market pilot (Session 2, Fall 2026):** Chicago, Boston (two campuses), Greenwich (Armonk). At launch capacity: 1,256 enrolled, $28.26M revenue, $23.6M site profit. From Session 3 (January 2027), the Chicago expansion steps the pilot to 2,948 enrolled, $66.33M revenue, ~$55.5M net. Year 1 ceiling ~$45M revenue, ~$38M net.
- **NYC is deliberately excluded until Fall 2027**, when New York opens at 125 seats with a proven playbook.
- **Full ~40-campus network at capacity:** 11,888 enrolled, $267.5M revenue, ~$219M net, about 1.7% of a ~$16B serviceable market inside a ~$40B total market. The middle and high school block lifts the ceiling by 50 percent from Fall 2027.
