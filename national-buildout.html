TAM: what US families already spend on tutoring ($18.9B) and after-school ($21.0B) combined, before any category growth. SAM: the share of that spend in Alpha's metros, roughly 40% of premium households. Today's entire network at full capacity on K-8 blocks is about 1.7% of the serviceable market. The bars are not to scale; if they were, today's slice would be a hairline. Alpha adds roughly 20 campuses a year, so the number climbs toward the market, not toward a cap.

Today's network at full capacity is a rounding error against the market. The constraint is buildings, not demand, and Alpha is opening about 20 a year. Alpha Hours grows with every one, and the middle and high school block lifts every participating campus ceiling by 50% from Fall 2027, a path toward $400M+.

Capacities are room capacity per block (enrolled is four cohorts on the K-8 blocks, room capacity x 4, from two weekday tracks and two Saturday blocks): Austin 300, Chicago 517 from January 2027, Miami 150, New York 125, Boston 100 and 40, Greenwich 80, Palm Beach 60, Miami Beach 50, all others a conservative 50. Premium pricing of $4,500 per session ($22,500 per year) at every campus. New York opens Fall 2027; its room is deliberately sized at 125 because the building also carries Founders and other campus programming in the same hours. Figures count K-8 blocks only; the 6:15 to 8:15 pm middle and high school block adds two more cohorts per participating campus from Fall 2027. Feeder pipeline is future Alpha tuition created at the modeled 8 to 12 percent conversion, not booked revenue. Market sizing reflects current IBISWorld and Afterschool Alliance spend estimates.
