Alpha Hours

The Alpha day, mastery-based academics on Timeback plus a life skills workshop, run after school and on Saturdays, in the buildings Alpha already pays for.

Nancy Wisniewski Torvund, proposed Head of Alpha Hours  ·  July 23, 2026

The idea

Run the Alpha day after school and on Saturdays, for families who do not yet attend Alpha and for current Alpha students whose school day ends at 3:30. A family picks a two-day track, Monday/Wednesday or Tuesday/Thursday, 4:00 to 6:00 pm, or a single four-hour Saturday block, inside Alpha campuses that sit empty after the school day. Either way the child gets 100 minutes of focused mastery academics on Timeback and 80 minutes of life skills a week, supported by guides hired and trained just for this program. Families enroll one seven-week session at a time, five sessions a year, each ending in an AlphaTest Showcase in front of the parents.

Premium per-session pricing, above every after-school competitor in the market.

It uses empty real estate

The building is a sunk cost Alpha already carries, owned or leased, with after-hours and weekend access either way. A late-afternoon or Saturday block adds near-zero facility cost.

The lowest-friction, paid way for a family to try the model, then enroll full-time at $40k to $75k. Conversion is the one modeled number, and the pilot exists to measure it.

It serves Alpha's own families

Every Alpha campus ends the day at 3:30 with nothing behind it. Current Alpha students transition directly on campus, at a 5% family discount.

How it runs

●The block starts where the Alpha day ends

Guide setup 3:30 to 3:45, drop-off 3:45 to 4:00, block 4:00 to 6:00 pm, dismissal 6:00 to 6:15. Every child is home for dinner. Saturdays run two four-hour blocks.

●Four cohorts share every room

Monday/Wednesday, Tuesday/Thursday, Saturday morning, Saturday afternoon. Enrolled per campus is four times room capacity, and the middle and high school block (6:15 to 8:15 pm) lifts that to six times from Fall 2027.

●Five sessions, one at a time, then the seat reprices

Each session is about seven weeks with a one-session minimum. Continuing past five sessions requires an Alpha application on file and the continuation rate ($6,500 premium / $5,000 standard), so a long-stayer is either pipeline or premium revenue.

●Every session ends on a stage

The AlphaTest Showcase tests students on the session's life skills in front of their parents, in the same week the parents receive the child's mastery map and weeks-to-grade-level report.

Why these campuses  ·  three markets, four campuses

The pilot is Chicago, Boston (two campuses), and Greenwich (Armonk). NYC is deliberately excluded until Fall 2027.

●Chicago tests the model at national scale

The network's largest campus: 94 seats at launch, 517 from January 2027, 2,068 enrolled at capacity. Its mid-pilot expansion is a live rehearsal of scaling a site, in a market outside the East Coast corridor, so the measured conversion number generalizes.

●Greenwich and Boston carry the premium

Dense UHNW markets at premium tuition where every conversion is worth the most, close enough that one launch team and a shared guide pool cover three campuses.

●NYC is held back on purpose

The network's most valuable admissions pipeline and most visible market is not put at risk before the model is proven. New York opens Fall 2027 at 125 seats with a proven playbook.

●The pilot is a conversion experiment

Success is conversion at or above 8% and re-enrollment at or above 70%, measured as lift against a matched baseline through a six-stage funnel. Decision rule, pre-committed: below 6% pause, 6 to 10% measured pace, above 10% national go.
