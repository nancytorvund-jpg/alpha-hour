# Alpha Hours: The Ask

**Four decisions to launch Alpha Hours in New York on October 19, 2026, and $365,000 that Session 2 returns as $423,000 at a quarter full, $1,123,000 on the plan, and $1,919,000 at capacity: the first step toward a national business worth $268M a year in revenue and $216M a year in net profit.**

Prepared by Nancy Wisniewski Torvund, proposed Head of Alpha Hours. August 28, 2026.

---

## 1. The numbers: Session 2

**The investment is $365,000.** A $141,000 one-time launch budget (guide training, the New York filing, launch marketing, a loaner device pool, insurance deposits, Emporium inventory, the registration and payments stack) plus $224,000 for the Head and the Operations and Admin VP, fully loaded, from September 1 through December 18. It is the only money Alpha puts in; every later step is paid from the session before it. The itemized budget is in section 7.

**The return, in Session 2 alone, October 19 to December 18:**

| | Conservative | Plan | Aggressive |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fill of 130 seats, four cohorts each | 25 percent | 60 percent | 100 percent |
| Enrolled | 130 | 312 | 520 |
| Revenue, collected before October 19 | $585,000 | $1,404,000 | $2,340,000 |
| Session 2 site profit | $423,000 | $1,123,000 | $1,919,000 |
| Net of the $365,000 | $58,000 | $758,000 | $1,554,000 |

The conservative case is every cohort a quarter full, roughly where the cohort floor starts closing cohorts, and it still returns the whole investment inside the session. The plan case, 60 percent fill, is the number I am held to: $365,000 in, $1,123,000 back, $758,000 net, 3.1 times the money in nine weeks. The aggressive case is both buildings full. Payback in every case is October 19, the day tuition is collected, because the balance of every family's $4,500 is due before day one.

### When the cash goes out and comes back

- **September 1 to October 19.** Cash out before the first tuition dollar is about $210K: the $141K launch budget and about $70K of Head and VP pay. Pay runs weekly, two weeks in arrears, so five of the seven pre-launch weeks are paid out by October 19 and the rest lands after tuition is in. - **Before October 19.** The balance of every family's $4,500 is due before day one. On the plan case $1.40M is collected before the first block runs. At capacity, $2.34M.
- **October 19 to December 18.** Guides are paid weekly, two weeks in arrears: the two training weeks land October 19 and 26, after tuition is in, and the seven session weeks land November 2 through January 1. Timeback is billed per session, fixed cost runs monthly. About $0.28M goes out against $1.40M already in, and the last two weeks of payroll, about $50K of central and guide pay, lands after December 18 from cash collected in October. Re-enrollment for Session 3 opens the week of November 9, so Session 3 deposits arrive before Session 2 ends.

The program never needs working capital from the school after the launch budget, and no lease, build-out, or capital purchase is required. The buildings are already paid for.

### What Session 2 buys after Session 2

- **Session 3 (January 4, 2027).** Greenwich (80 seats) and Boston (40 and 100) open. Their launch cost, about $200K, is paid from Session 2 revenue. Five campuses, 350 seats, $31.5M run rate at capacity.
- **Session 4 (February 24, 2027).** Chicago (517), Miami (150), Palm Beach (60), Miami Beach (50). About $400K of launch cost, paid from Session 3. Nine campuses, 1,127 seats, 4,508 enrolled, $101.4M run rate, $83.5M net at capacity.
- **Year 1 (Sessions 2 to 5).** Plan $35.4M revenue, $28.2M net after the full $875K central team, leaving Session 5 at an $82M run rate. Capacity $49.2M / $39.9M. Floor $12.3M / $8.6M. Total one-time launch cost across the year is about $0.74M, all of it paid from the sessions before it. The $365,000 in this document is the only money that comes from Alpha.
- **Session 1, Fall 2027.** National launch, about 40 campuses, 11,908 children on Timeback after school, $268M revenue, about $216M net at capacity.

### The downside

Break-even is 15 students at 180 Maiden Lane and 12 at 156 William Street, 27 in total against 312 on the plan. The cohort floor decision on October 5 closes any cohort under ten seats two weeks out, so an empty cohort never opens. The most any site can lose is seven weeks of payroll. There is no lease, no build-out, and no device fleet to write off; the loaner pool is $16K.
