# BrainLift: Alpha Hours

Nancy Wisniewski Torvund

## Purpose

### Primary purpose

Design, launch and run Alpha Hours: a premium after-school program that delivers the Alpha day, mastery-based academics on Timeback, followed by a life skills workshop, to families who do not yet attend Alpha and current Alpha students whose school day ends at 3:30. Families attend four hours a week: a two-night track (Monday/Wednesday or Tuesday/Thursday, 4:00 to 6:00 pm) or a single four-hour Saturday block (two are offered), all inside existing Alpha campuses. Every block runs Timeback in pomodoros, 25-minute mastery sessions, ends with life skills and shoutouts, and families enroll one seven-week session at a time, five sessions a year.

### The program exists to do four things at once

1. Generate profit from a premium after-school offering priced above every competitor.
2. Use real estate we already pay for, turning empty late-afternoon, evening and weekend hours in Alpha buildings into revenue.
3. Feed Alpha. Give families a low-friction, paid way to try the Alpha model, and convert them into full-time enrollment. Conversion is the one modeled number in this plan, and Phase 1 exists to replace it with a measured one.
4. Serve Alpha's own families. Almost every Alpha campus in the country ends the school day at 3:30 with nothing behind it. Alpha Hours closes that gap on every campus, and gets paid a premium price to do it. Current Alpha students enroll at the same session price with a 5 percent family discount, and transition directly on campus at the end of the school day.

Success is measured on all four: site profit, building utilization, conversions to full Alpha, and current-family enrollment. Underneath them sit Alpha's three commitments, and every one carries into Alpha Hours with its own measurement.

**Kids love school.** Underneath everything sits Alpha's first commitment, kids love school, and it carries into Alpha Hours unchanged: children love coming. This is measured by session-to-session re-enrollment and attendance, in addition to student surveys, and it is the leading indicator for everything else. A child who begs to come back is what fills the cohorts, climbs the mastery data, and converts the family. Profit and conversion are the outcomes, love of the program is the engine, and we track it accordingly.

**Learn 2x.** Students learn at the accelerated pace Alpha holds itself to during their academic minutes. Measured by following every student's academic progress and learning velocity in Timeback, session over session. We do not promise 2x learning in one session, but promise the roadmap to 2x learning should the student enroll full-time at an Alpha School.

**Learn life skills.** Every session culminates in an AlphaTest Showcase, where students are tested on the life skills they have been working on, in front of their parents. The students will already be convinced by the time they have the showcase, it exists as a mechanism for convincing the parents.

### Out of scope

- Custom changes to Timeback for after-school use. The program runs the platform as built, not subject-by-subject.
- Certified-teacher tutoring models or one-on-one private tutoring. Alpha Hours cannot accommodate students who need 1:1 support, and says so publicly on the landing page.
- Test-prep-only offerings (SAT and ACT, APs).
- Non-academic enrichment (arts, sports, social clubs) as the core product.
- Full-day or full-time schooling. This is strictly after-school and Saturday.
- Online-only delivery. The program is in-person inside Alpha buildings.
- Childcare or custodial care. Alpha Hours is academic instruction, structured to qualify as instruction under state exemptions.
- Subjects beyond Timeback. Grades outside K-8 at launch; a dedicated middle and high school evening block launches Fall 2027.

## Spiky Points of View

### SPOV 1: It's bad business to give away all of our trials for free when we can make money on them.

Traditional school admissions gives free trials to children because the schools need the families more than the families need the school. Alpha Schools do this today, too, with free shadow days, staff time and resources given away. Alpha Hours inverts this. Alpha Hours funnels a portion of prospective families into a paid experience, monetizing the trial that otherwise would have been free. There's an opportunity to make money here that we shouldn't throw away.

### SPOV 2: Every pathway to yes outside of Alpha is losing us money.

We can monetize our "pathway to yes." At Alpha a no is never a no forever. For every student who is not admitted, it is a "not yet" with a pathway to yes. Currently, the pathway to yes is feedback and roadmap. We can take that and turn it into a monetized pathway to yes inside of Alpha. Students who are a "not now" for Alpha Schools can join Alpha Hours with clear feedback and a roadway that they pay for inside of Alpha, and where we can more thoroughly evaluate the child and his or her progress.

### SPOV 3: A pathway to yes with no way to measure it or keep the families in our funnel is worthless.

We can make our "pathway to yes" concrete and measurable, generating future revenue. Our current pathway to yes is feedback and a roadmap. We can take this and make it concrete and measurable, while gathering more detailed data over time on each student.

### SPOV 4: One shadow day is inferior to seven weeks of data.

Some students are stand out "yeses" on a shadow day, but many are not. The data we gather in the course of a few hours is inferior to data gathered over seven weeks. We can boost the quality of our student population by using Alpha Hours to gather more detailed data on students who are not stand out "yeses."

### SPOV 5: Alpha Hours is a stronger qualifier than $100 and some unmeasured level of interest.

Currently, families apply with a $100 application fee, and are handed a free Alpha experience. $100 does not indicate ability or willingness to pay $40,000-75,000 per year. Alpha Hours would be our most powerful qualifier for families. The families are willing to invest $4,500 and four hours a week for seven weeks. This tells us that a family has a strong level of interest, and is willing to pay.

### SPOV 6: 100 minutes a week and a roadmap is superior to six hours a day and a letter grade.

What 100 weekly minutes of Timeback produces something no school or human teacher in America can give a parent, a precise mastery map of where the child actually is in every subject, the child's measured learning velocity, and a computed answer to the only question that matters, which is exactly how many weeks at full Alpha until this child is at grade level, or the next one. The traditional school has the child six hours a day for years and produces a report card guessing subjectively at all of this. Seven weeks with us produces the map and the timeline. Every family who pays for Alpha Hours is running an experiment that indicts the school they already write checks to. We do not compete with Kumon. We compete with six hours of no information, and we win in seven weeks.

### SPOV 7: Every building sitting empty after 3:30 PM is a waste of assets that we should be sweating.

We have buildings, owned or leased, across the country that are sitting empty for the majority of hours in the week. We should be sweating our assets to maximum capacity. Alpha Hours is just the start of a path to making money on every single available hour.

## Insights

### Insight 1: Alpha Hours turns admissions from a cost center into a P&L.

Once trials are paid and the pathway to yes is monetized, admissions stops being pure spend: shadow-day staff time becomes billable program hours and the marketing dollars behind every inquiry are recovered inside the funnel itself. This only happens if routing is policy, not improvisation. Admissions needs a formal disposition step for every family: yes, not yet with an Alpha Hours roadmap, or no. The moment routing is left to individual judgment, the revenue leaks back to free shadow days.

### Insight 2: The "not yet" path to yes letter becomes a product.

Today a not yet costs goodwill and loses the family to another school's funnel. A not yet that ships with an Alpha Hours seat, a concrete roadmap of mastery targets, and a scheduled re-evaluation date converts a rejection into revenue, retention, and a second look on better data. The roadmap must name measurable thresholds, so re-application becomes a data check instead of a second subjective judgment.

### Insight 3: Seven weeks of data should produce the admissions decision, so set the bar before the session starts.

Before each session opens, we define exactly what a child needs to show to earn an invitation to apply: how much they mastered, how consistently they showed up and completed their sessions. Then the decision makes itself. By the end of seven weeks, the data says yes or not yet, the same way the week-1 exit process works: the standard is set in advance and the record makes the call, not a gut feeling. This is what makes "a more thorough evaluation" a real claim instead of a longer version of the same judgment call. And it means we can raise the quality of the students we admit without admitting fewer of them.

### Insight 4: Alpha Hours families are the most qualified families in the funnel, so measure them differently.

A family that pays $4,500 and commits four hours a week for seven weeks has told us far more than a family that pays $100 and fills out a form. Alpha Hours does not replace the existing funnel, it adds a layer of deeply qualified families on top of it. But those families cannot be measured with the funnel's old yardstick. If we count them the way we count applications, the numbers will understate what the program is doing. The measures that capture it: qualified families enrolled in the program, conversion to full Alpha by number of sessions completed, and cost per enrollment, which turns negative because the family pays us while they decide.

### Insight 5: The data builds over time, and it moves with the child.

A shadow day gives us a few hours of impressions, and they fade. Alpha Hours gives us weeks of real data on what the child has mastered and the life skills they learn, and the data grows every session. When that family enrolls at full Alpha, the data comes with them: the child starts on day one at exactly the right level, no testing, no ramp-up. The same data that tells us whether to admit the child also tells us where to start them. No other school's trial produces either.

### Insight 6: Sweating the assets has an order of operations. Alpha Hours is the first step in filling every empty hour, and each step after it gets cheaper.

Alpha campuses sit empty most hours of the week. Alpha Hours fills the first slice, weekday afternoons and Saturdays, and in doing so puts a trained team, insurance, and an operating playbook in place at every campus, all carried by that first program. Once that foundation exists, every other empty block, early mornings, school-holiday weeks, summer gaps, adult evenings, can be added without rebuilding it, so each new program starts at a higher margin than the one before it. Alpha Hours is not the whole building-utilization strategy. It is just the first block of it.

### Insight 7: A monetized pathway to yes must never pay more than the yes.

Alpha Hours earns two ways: session fees of about $22,500 a year, and conversions to full Alpha worth $40,000 to $75,000 a year. The conversion is worth far more, but the session fee is the one the program books on its own P&L, so whoever runs Alpha Hours has a built-in temptation to keep families enrolled as "not yet" instead of pushing them to full Alpha. Left alone, that turns the feeder into a tutoring business that competes with the school it was built to fill. Three structures prevent it: the review thresholds that trigger the conversion conversation are published in advance, so the invitation fires on data, not on the Head's judgment; every family gets a scheduled re-evaluation each session, so no one sits in limbo; and any family continuing past five sessions must have an Alpha application on file and pays the significantly higher continuation rate, so a long-stayer is either pipeline or premium revenue, never a parked seat. The last piece is accountability. The Head is reviewed on conversion rate and full-time enrollments, not on fill rate or session revenue, so the person running the program can only win by doing what the school needs.

## Knowledge Tree

### Category 1: How non-school learning environments drive academic gains

Summary: Mastery pedagogy, standardized pacing, and visible progress let after school programs outperform classrooms, using process-driven personalization rather than teacher variation.

- Kumon: Founded 1954. Worksheet-based mastery, non-certified staff, 25,000+ centers, $150 to $220 per subject per month. Personalization lives in the process (worksheets and standards), not the content.
- Mathnasium: Founded 2002. Trained non-teacher instructors, fixed method, math only, $250 to $500 per month.
- Fusion Academy: One-to-one private school, $8,000 to $17,000 per course per year. Proves premium willingness to pay for personalized academics.
- Alpha / Timeback / Alpha Hours: Leverages AI to deliver personalized academics equivalent/superior to 1:1 tutoring with a human, keeping each child in his or her zone of proximal development in each subject, delivering 2x learning.

Insights: Mastery scales when the system, not the teacher, carries instruction. Parents validate trust through visible, measurable progress. Premium after-school is about quality of result, not quantity of hours. Alpha Hours will provide personalized academic reports at the start and end of every session.

### Category 2: After-school business models and the feeder

Summary: After-school programs scale through standardization, real-estate leverage, and, for a school-owned program, conversion to full enrollment.

Market size: US families already spend about $40B a year on tutoring and after-school combined: tutoring is an $18.9B industry growing about 11 percent annually with K-5 the fastest segment (IBISWorld 2025); after-school is $21.0B, with 7M children enrolled and 22.6M more whose families want a spot and cannot get one (Afterschool Alliance 2025). Tutoring participation rose from 19 to 24 percent of students between 2024 and 2025, and high-income families already spend up to $9,000 per child per year on tutoring and enrichment. Alpha's full 40-campus network at capacity is $267M, well under 1 percent of what families already spend today. The constraint is buildings, not demand.

Demand evidence, bottom-up. After-school is unprompted inbound demand, not only a hypothesis. In NYC admissions conversations, families ask whether Alpha offers after-school without being prompted. In the last 14 days, 3 of 23 NYC admissions conversations included an unprompted ask for after-school, 13 percent of all conversations, with no marketing and no product to point to. Most recently last week on Wednesday, a family said they would continue with Alpha by trying it that way if after school existed. The same pattern held in my prior private-school sales role, where the after-school question came up at least biweekly across a national territory. The demand also runs inside our own walls: Alpha parents nationwide ask what happens after the school day ends at 3:30, and today the answer is nothing. These are families telling us the entry product they want to buy before we have built it. Price acceptance is already proven at this exact number: Alpha Summer in the Hamptons has 174 paid registrations at $4,500 per week, with one price objection total. Alpha Hours asks the same families the same number for seven weeks instead of one.

- Huntington: Founded 1977. Diagnostic-driven, multi-subject, $40 to $75 per hour. The assessments build trust and justify the price.
- Premium pricing precedent: Command Education $120,000 a year, up to $750,000 for a multi-year package; Lindamood-Bell $10,000+ for six weeks; Fusion $60,000+ full-time. All price against outcome value, not hours, and all quote the package, not the hour.
- Feeder economics: A private school typically spends $3,700 to $5,800 in marketing to win one enrolled student (Cube Creative Design, 2026 Private School Marketing Budget Planning Guide: industry median $3,677, secondary $5,844). Alpha Hours flips that: the prospective family pays us $4,500 a session while they decide, so evaluating Alpha earns money instead of costing it. The trial is also far stronger than a shadow day. Every session ends with a conversion artifact. The child's mastery map and a personalized projection of weeks-to-grade-level at full Alpha, so the parent leaves holding a number, not an impression. Private schools typically convert 5 to 8 percent of inquiries to enrollments (Spiral Marketing, day-school benchmarks), and an inquiry is a stranger with a phone number, while every Alpha Hours family has paid $4,500 and watched their own child's mastery data climb for seven weeks. We model conversion at 8 to 12 percent, and Phase 1 exists to replace that assumption with our own measured number. Each conversion is worth $160,000 to $325,000 in lifetime tuition, which is why the feeder pipeline ($230M to $340M nationally) is worth more than the program's own profit.

Insights: Standardization plus assessment builds scalable trust. Real estate already controlled is the margin. For a school-owned program, the feeder is the largest source of value, and it only functions with an intentional, structured handoff.

## Business Model and Operations

### The program

An Alpha day runs primarily on two things: mastery-based academics on Timeback and life skills workshops. Alpha Hours compresses that whole day into one or two blocks. A weekday block is two hours, and requires a Monday/Wednesday or Tuesday/Thursday registration. Each weekday block is organized as this: a ten-minute Limitless launch, two 25-minute academic sessions on Timeback with a short break between, a 40-minute life skills workshop, and ten minutes of shoutouts. Each of the two Saturday blocks runs the same day at double length: four 25-minute academic sessions, an 80-minute workshop, and a snack break in the middle. Either way the child gets the same week: 100 minutes of focused, adaptive mastery academics and 80 minutes of life skills. Timeback delivers the instruction and adapts to each child. The guide holds focus, effort, and motivation, and leads the workshop. That is the whole model: the Alpha day, academics and life skills, in one or two blocks.

### Motivation and incentives

Alpha Hours runs the same rhythm as Alpha, starting with academics in Timeback, followed by a life skills workshop. What is different from Alpha is the child's day. The child has already been through six hours of regular school, and walks in tired at 4 pm, and needs a reason to give full effort. Alpha already has this reason, built into the model twice. The first is Timeback's own promise, carried into every block: get through your academics with focus, and the rest of the block is an amazing life skills workshop, the part of the block built around things kids actually love. The academics are the shortest path to the good part, exactly the trade Alpha kids make every day. The second is the extrinsic motivational model, and we will mirror this in Alpha Hours. Students will earn the Alpha currency for XP earned in Timeback, and will have the opportunity to purchase items in the Alpha Hours Emporium. Both motivators become even more important for a child who has been through six hours of school: they keep the child working diligently, and boost love of Alpha Hours.

### AlphaTest Showcase

Every session ends with an AlphaTest Showcase in the final week. Students are tested on the life skills they have worked on across the session's workshops, and parents come to see it. The showcase does double duty: it is how the life skills commitment gets measured instead of assumed, and it puts every parent in the building in the same week they receive their child's mastery map and weeks-to-grade-level report, which makes it the natural setting for the re-enrollment and full-Alpha conversation. No other after-school program ends a term by putting the child's growth on a stage in front of the person who paid for it.

### Schedule and tracks

Runs Monday through Thursday afternoons and Saturday, inside existing Alpha campuses. The weekday block starts right where the Alpha school day ends, so the building never goes dark and every child is home for dinner.

Weekday block rundown (inside the two hours): Limitless launch: 10 minutes. Academic session 1 (25 minutes). Break: 5 minutes. Academic session 2 (25 minutes). Transition: 5 minutes. Life skills workshop: 40 minutes. Shoutouts: 10 minutes. Total 120 minutes.

Saturday schedule: Setup: 8:45 to 9:00 am. AM block: 9:00 am to 1:00 pm. Transition: 1:00 to 2:00 pm (staggered pickup and drop-off; no lunch served). PM block: 2:00 to 6:00 pm. Clean up: 6:00 to 6:15 pm.

Saturday block rundown (inside the four hours): Limitless launch: 10 minutes. Academic session 1 (25 minutes). Break: 5 minutes. Academic session 2 (25 minutes). Break: 15 minutes. Academic session 3 (25 minutes). Break: 5 minutes. Academic session 4 (25 minutes). Snack break: 15 minutes. Life skills workshop: 80 minutes. Shoutouts: 10 minutes. Total 240 minutes. Four academic sessions. Snacks only, no meals.

Every campus runs four cohorts a week: Monday/Wednesday, Tuesday/Thursday, Saturday morning, and Saturday afternoon. The same room and the same guide team serve all four, and every cohort gets the same four weekly hours.

Middle and high school block (launches Fall 2027): Weekdays 6:15 to 8:15 pm, Monday/Wednesday or Tuesday/Thursday, drop-off 6:00 to 6:15, dismissal 8:15 to 8:30. The block adds two cohorts per campus and lifts the enrolled ceiling from four times room capacity to six. Staffing runs as a second part-time guide rotation, sized per campus when the block launches.

### Capacity

Room capacity is the seats filled at one time, set per campus. Enrolled students per campus is four times the room capacity: two weekday tracks plus two Saturday blocks. A 100-seat room fills to 400 enrolled students. From Fall 2027, the middle and high school block adds two more weekday cohorts, lifting the ceiling to six times room capacity. The operating goal is to fill every cohort to capacity.

### Enrollment

Five sessions a year, each about seven weeks (about 35 program weeks). Families sign up one session at a time, choosing a weekday track or a Saturday block, with a one-session minimum. They re-enroll session to session. Current Alpha students enroll on the same terms at the same session price with a 5 percent family discount. Prospective families enroll at the standard rate for any five sessions, consecutive or not, essentially a full program year. Continuing past five requires a completed Alpha application on file and the continuation rate: $6,500 per session in premium markets, $5,000 standard. No family is exited for staying, the seat reprices and the family enters the admissions funnel by definition. Where a cohort holds a waitlist, families with applications on file hold their seats and families without release them first. Current Alpha students are exempt. One session at a time lowers the barrier to trying Alpha and cycles far more distinct families through each seat than an annual plan.

### Real estate

The building is a sunk cost Alpha already carries, whether it owns the campus or leases it, with after-hours and weekend access either way. Late-afternoon and Saturday use adds only marginal cost. Marginal cost about $150 to $250 per weekday, about $400 per Saturday (custodial, HVAC and lighting, security). Because the host is an Alpha campus, the facility fee is largely intracompany.

A single session is four to five times the most expensive mainstream competitor for the same span. The full year is roughly a third (30 to 40 percent) of full Alpha tuition ($40,000 to $75,000). We quote the session and full-year numbers, not the hour.

The price point is already market-tested: 174 paid Alpha Summer registrations at $4,500 per week, one price objection in 174 transactions. At $4,500 per seven-week session, Alpha Hours is the same number for seven times the duration.

## Financials

### Unit economics per campus

Revenue = enrolled x price per session x 5 sessions. (Enrolled = room capacity x 4.)

Per-student cost per year: Timeback $150 x 5 sessions, supplies and rewards $30 x 5, payment processing 3 percent, local marketing $300. About $1,875 per student per year.

Guides: 1 per 10 concurrent seats, part-time at $50,000 (roughly 21 hours a week). The same guide serves all four cohorts, so one guide supports up to 40 enrolled students, about $1,250 of guide cost per enrolled student at even fill.

Per-campus fixed cost: Lead Guide $75,000, Campus Coordinator $50,000 (50 percent role), facility about $45,000 a year, insurance about $8,000.

Margin runs about 83 percent at capacity, higher at larger campuses where the fixed team spreads over more revenue.

### Downside economics

Contribution per enrolled student is about $20,625 a year ($4,500 per session less about $375 in variable cost, times five sessions). Minimum operating cost per campus is about $278K, so break-even is 14 enrolled students per campus, about 4 per cohort, under 4 percent of full capacity at the largest sites. Enrollment closes before each session starts and guides are hired to sold seats, so every staffing step is taken against booked revenue, and site margin holds between 70 and 83 percent at every fill level above break-even. Exposure is capped by design: one-session enrollment, part-time staff, no capex, and a building Alpha already pays for mean the maximum downside at any site is seven weeks of payroll.

Two mechanical floors sit under it. Cohort floor: a cohort that has not sold 10 seats two weeks before session start does not open, and its families roll into the other weekday track or a Saturday block. Site floor: a campus below break-even at week 3 of Session 2 closes at session end, staff released, room returned to the campus. Week 3 is the checkpoint because attendance and re-enrollment intent, the program's own leading indicators, are both visible by then.

Year 1 is four sessions, Sessions 2 through 5, one at launch capacity and three at expanded, so the Year 1 ceiling is about $45M in pilot revenue and roughly $38M net at full fill. No fill forecast is offered, because fill is one of the things Session 2 exists to measure, and the floors above cap the downside at every level. ## Potential Objections and Challenges

### Reputation

Alpha Hours will be many families' first Alpha experience, and it carries the school's brand into every one of them. A weak session damages the school and the feeder, not just the program. This is why NYC, the network's most valuable pipeline and most visible market, is held out of the pilot entirely and opens Fall 2027 with a proven playbook. Inside the pilot, quality is carried by the guide training program and by staffing guides to sold seats, so no room ever opens understaffed. The Lead Guide owns quality at every site, the program runs Alpha's own standards and rituals, and the AlphaTest Showcase puts the proof on a stage every seven weeks.

### Wrong-fit students, and the week 1 exit process

One student who cannot function in a 1:10 room destroys the product for the nine other families who paid $4,500, and at this price that is not survivable. So the exit trigger is behavioral and mechanical, not a judgment call. Two things trigger it: behavior, every incident that pulls a guide off the room, disrupting another student's session, refusing to remain in the block, is logged the moment it happens, and two logged incidents in week 1 is an exit before week 2. And level of support, a child who requires a level of individual support that compromises the program's ability to deliver the three commitments, kids love school, learn 2x, learn life skills, to the rest of the room. The Head of Alpha Hours delivers the decision to the family, the log makes it. The family receives the incident record and a prorated refund. Both standards are published at enrollment, so an exit is the program enforcing what every family in the room paid for.

### Students who need 1:1 support

The staffing model is 1 guide per 10 students, and it cannot deliver one-to-one support. This is publicly listed on the landing page before any family pays: Alpha Hours cannot accommodate students who need 1:1 support. Stating it up front protects the ratio, sets expectations honestly, and filters at the top of the funnel instead of in week 1.

### The arrival window for non-Alpha students

Outside schools dismiss around 3:00, and drop-off closes at 4:00. For most target families this is routine, and for the rest the two Saturday blocks carry the load. The window is held at 15 minutes on purpose: the block starts on time for everyone, every time.

### Saturday competes with youth sports

Saturday morning is prime league time in exactly our demographic. Two Saturday blocks, two weekday tracks, and session-at-a-time enrollment let families rotate around sports seasons instead of choosing against them permanently.

### The parking risk

Some families will treat Alpha Hours as Alpha at a third of the price and delay full enrollment. The structured conversion conversation, the mastery map, and the weeks-to-grade-level projection exist to make the ceiling explicit: 100 minutes a week produces the map, full Alpha produces the acceleration. And parking now pays or converts: any family past five sessions holds an Alpha application and pays the continuation rate, $6,500 a session for premium markets, $5,000 for other markets, so a long-stayer is either pipeline by definition or premium revenue, and where a waitlist stands, non-applicants release the seat first.

### Guide supply at national scale

Guide hiring and training are owned by the Head of Alpha Hours. The candidate pool, public school teachers and retired teachers, exists at scale in every metro, and the Alpha Hours training program, shadowing at Alpha NYC plus AI-first asynchronous training, standardizes quality before any guide runs a room.

## Rollout

### Phase 1 (Session 2, Fall 2026): three-market pilot, run as a conversion experiment

Three markets, four campuses: Chicago, Boston (two sites), Greenwich (Armonk). Every campus opens at full capacity, all four cohorts on sale, no cap and no small-group phase: the pilot enrolls every family these markets will buy. Chicago opens on its current 94-seat footprint, 376 enrolled at capacity, ahead of the campus expansion in Phase 2. The floors in Financials carry the downside, and quality is carried by the guide training program, the Lead Guide at every site, and guides hired to sold seats so no room opens understaffed. NYC is deliberately not in the pilot: it is the network's most valuable admissions pipeline and its most visible market, and the program does not put either at risk before the model is proven. New York gets Alpha Hours in Fall 2027, with a proven playbook. Launching at Session 2 rather than Session 1 gives campuses a settled start to the school year and the runway for guide hiring, state exemption determinations (Illinois, Massachusetts, Connecticut), and insurance.

### Pilot success criteria and measurement plan

The pilot succeeds on two measured numbers, not revenue: conversion to full Alpha at or above 8 percent, and session-over-session re-enrollment at or above 70 percent. The experiment is instrumented before it opens, in five commitments.

1. The funnel is defined before Session 2 starts. Conversion is not one number; it is tracked per family through six stages: paid Alpha Hours enrollment, structured conversion conversation held, application submitted, admitted, full-Alpha enrollment signed, first tuition payment. Stage-level tracking is what says whether a weak result is a marketing problem, an admissions problem, or a product problem.
2. The trigger is mechanical. The thresholds that fire the conversion conversation and the application invitation, mastery gains, and session completion rate, are published before Session 2 opens, so the measured rate reflects the product, not the persuasiveness of whichever Lead Guide ran the conversation that week.
3. The number is lift, not a raw count. Conversion is measured against a matched baseline of non-Alpha-Hours inquiries from the same markets and the same window, because some share of these families would have applied anyway, and the case rests on what Alpha Hours adds, not on conversions that were coming regardless.
4. Sample size is respected. Session 2's 1,256 families are reported as directional, with the margin of error stated; the go decision runs on the full 2,948 base by Session 3. Conversion is reported by campus, by cohort, by session count, and by market, and a material spread between Chicago and the East Coast markets is treated as a market-mix finding that reshapes rollout sequencing, not noise to average away.
5. The decision rule is pre-committed. Below 6 percent, national rollout and New York pause while the funnel stages say where it broke. Six to 10 percent, the rollout proceeds at a measured regional pace. Above 10 percent, full national go. Session 3's leadership conversation is the number and what it triggers, not another debate.

### Phase 2 (Session 3, January 2027): the Chicago expansion

Chicago's campus expansion lands and Alpha Hours steps with it, from 94 seats to 517, lifting the enrolled ceiling from 376 to 2,068 and the pilot from 1,256 to 2,948. One session of Chicago operating data, a waitlist built against the 94-seat footprint, and a trained guide bench sized to sold seats carry the step. This is the national build in miniature: the same room, the same playbook, five and a half times the seats, executed mid-pilot.

### Phase 3 (Session 4, Spring 2027): national launch

The playbook rolls across the country with two full sessions of operating data and the first measured conversion numbers. All campuses join here, with the exemption playbook in hand.

### Phase 4 (Fall 2027): New York, and the middle and high school block

Alpha Hours opens at Alpha New York with a year of operating data and a proven playbook, entering the network's most valuable market at full strength. NYC opens at 125 seats, 500 enrolled at capacity: the floor holds 190, but the room is deliberately sized at 125 because the building also carries Founders and other campus programming in the same hours. The same season, the 6:15 to 8:15 pm block launches, academics on Timeback with Alpha Max and Alpha X programming filling the workshop time, lifting every participating campus ceiling by 50 percent. The network stands at about 40 campuses and grows with every new campus Alpha opens.

### Why these campuses

Chicago is the network's largest campus: 94 seats at launch, 517 from January 2027, 2,068 enrolled at capacity, so the pilot tests the model at the exact scale the national build depends on, and its mid-pilot expansion is a live rehearsal of scaling a site, in a market outside the East Coast corridor, which makes the measured conversion number generalize. Greenwich and Boston are dense UHNW markets at premium tuition where every conversion is worth the most, close enough that one launch team and a shared guide pool cover three campuses. NYC is excluded on purpose: the admissions pipeline there is already strong and the brand risk is highest, so New York opens Fall 2027, after the model is proven.
