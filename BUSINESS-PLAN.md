## 1. What Alpha Hours is

Alpha Hours is a paid, premium after-school and Saturday program that runs inside Alpha School buildings after the regular school day ends. It delivers the Alpha day, mastery-based academics on Timeback followed by a life skills workshop, to two groups: families who do not yet attend Alpha, and current Alpha students whose school day ends at 3:30. The child works on Timeback, Alpha's AI mastery platform, supported in person by guides hired and trained specifically for this program.

The program does four jobs at once:

1. **It makes money.** Families pay a premium price per session, above every after-school competitor in the market.
2. **It uses real estate we already pay for.** Alpha campuses sit empty in the late afternoon and on weekends. Alpha Hours turns those hours into revenue at close to zero added facility cost.
3. **It feeds Alpha.** Alpha Hours is the lowest-friction, paid way for a family to try the Alpha model without leaving their current school. A share of those families enroll full-time, and full Alpha tuition runs $40,000 to $75,000 per year. Conversion is the one modeled number in this plan, and Phase 1 exists to replace it with a measured one.
4. **It serves Alpha's own families.** Almost every Alpha campus in the country ends the school day at 3:30 with nothing behind it. Alpha Hours closes that gap on every campus, and gets paid a premium price to do it. Current Alpha students enroll at the same session price with a 5 percent family discount and transition directly on campus at the end of the school day.

Success is measured on all four: site profit, building utilization, conversions to full Alpha, and current-family enrollment. Underneath them sit Alpha's three commitments, each with its own measurement in Alpha Hours: kids love school (re-enrollment, attendance, and student surveys), learn 2x (every student's progress and learning velocity tracked in Timeback, session over session), and learn life skills (the AlphaTest Showcase that ends every session). Alpha's accelerated-learning figures are Alpha's own internal data.

Start market: grades K-8 at established Alpha campuses, scaling nationwide across the Alpha network. A dedicated middle and high school evening block launches Fall 2027.

## 2. The program model

### The Alpha day, after school

An Alpha day runs primarily on two things: mastery-based academics on Timeback and life skills workshops. Alpha Hours compresses that whole day into one or two blocks. A weekday block is two hours: a ten-minute Limitless launch, two 25-minute academic sessions on Timeback with a short break between, a 40-minute life skills workshop, and ten minutes of shoutouts. Each of the two Saturday blocks runs the same day at double length: four 25-minute academic sessions, an 80-minute workshop, and a snack break in the middle. Either way the child gets the same week: 100 minutes of focused, adaptive mastery academics and 80 minutes of life skills. Timeback delivers the instruction and adapts to each child. The guide holds focus, effort, and motivation, and leads the workshop. That is the whole model: the Alpha day, academics and life skills, in one or two blocks.

### Motivation and incentives

The child in Alpha Hours has already been through six hours of regular school and walks in tired at 4 pm, so the block needs a built-in reason to give full effort. Alpha already has this reason, built into the model twice. First, Timeback's own promise carries into every block: get through your academics with focus, and the rest of the block is the life skills workshop, the part built around things kids actually love. The academics are the shortest path to the good part, exactly the trade Alpha kids make every day. Second, the extrinsic model: students earn the Alpha currency for XP earned in Timeback and can spend it in the Alpha Hours Emporium. Both motivators keep a tired child working diligently and build love of the program.

### AlphaTest Showcase

Every session ends with an AlphaTest Showcase in the final week. Students are tested on the life skills they have worked on across the session's workshops, and parents come to see it. The Showcase does double duty: it is how the life skills commitment gets measured instead of assumed, and it puts every parent in the building in the same week they receive their child's mastery map and weeks-to-grade-level report, which makes it the natural setting for the re-enrollment and full-Alpha conversation.

### Schedule and tracks

Alpha Hours runs Monday through Thursday afternoons and Saturday, inside existing Alpha campuses. The weekday block starts right where the Alpha school day ends, so the building never goes dark and every child is home for dinner. Each family picks a **two-day weekday track, Monday and Wednesday or Tuesday and Thursday**, or a **single Saturday block, morning or afternoon**.

Every campus runs four cohorts a week: Monday/Wednesday, Tuesday/Thursday, Saturday morning, and Saturday afternoon. The same room and the same guide team serve all four, and every cohort gets the same four weekly hours.

**Middle and high school block (launches Fall 2027):** weekdays 6:15 to 8:15 pm, Monday/Wednesday or Tuesday/Thursday, drop-off 6:00 to 6:15, dismissal 8:15 to 8:30. Same spine as the K-8 block: Limitless launch, two 25-minute academic sessions on Timeback, then Alpha Max and Alpha X programming fills the workshop slot, shoutouts to close. The block adds two cohorts per campus and lifts the enrolled ceiling from four times room capacity to six. Staffing runs as a second part-time guide rotation, sized per campus when the block launches.

### Capacity

Room capacity is the seats filled at one time, set per campus. Enrolled students per campus is four times the room capacity: two weekday tracks plus two Saturday blocks. A 100-seat room fills to 400 enrolled students. From Fall 2027, the middle and high school block adds two more weekday cohorts, lifting the ceiling to six times room capacity. The operating goal is to fill every cohort to capacity.

### Enrollment: one session at a time

The year runs **five sessions**, each about seven weeks (about 35 program weeks). Families sign up **one session at a time**, choosing a weekday track or a Saturday block, with a **one-session minimum**, and re-enroll session to session. Current Alpha students enroll on the same terms at the same session price with a 5 percent family discount.

Prospective families enroll at the standard rate for any five sessions, consecutive or not, essentially a full program year. **Continuing past five sessions requires a completed Alpha application on file and the continuation rate: $6,500 per session in premium markets, $5,000 standard.** No family is exited for staying: the seat reprices and the family enters the admissions funnel by definition. Where a cohort holds a waitlist, families with applications on file hold their seats and families without release them first. Current Alpha students are exempt.

One session at a time lowers the barrier to trying Alpha and cycles far more distinct families through each seat than an annual plan. Every one of those families is a full-time Alpha prospect who has lived the model.

## 3. Market analysis

### The market

US families already spend about **$40 billion a year** on tutoring and after-school combined:

- Tutoring is an **$18.9 billion** industry growing about 11 percent annually, with K-5 the fastest segment (IBISWorld, 2025). Tutoring participation rose from 19 to 24 percent of students between 2024 and 2025.
- After-school is a **$21.0 billion** industry, with 7 million children enrolled and **22.6 million more** whose families want a spot and cannot get one (Afterschool Alliance, 2025). The industry is short on capacity, not demand.
- High-income families already spend up to about $9,000 per child per year on tutoring and enrichment.

| Layer | Definition | Size |
|---|---|---|
| **TAM** | What US families already spend on tutoring and after-school combined ($18.9B tutoring, $21.0B after-school), before any category growth | **~$40B / year** |
| **SAM** | The share of that spend in Alpha's metros, roughly 40 percent of premium households | **~$16B / year** |
| **SOM (today)** | The full 40-campus network at capacity on K-8 blocks (11,888 students, Section 7A) | **~$267M / year** |

The point is the ratio: the full network at capacity is about 1.7 percent of the serviceable market. Alpha Hours is not constrained by demand. It is constrained by buildings, and Alpha adds about 20 campuses a year.

### Demand evidence, bottom-up

After-school is unprompted inbound demand, not only a hypothesis. In NYC admissions conversations, families ask whether Alpha offers after-school without being prompted: in the last 14 days, 3 of 23 NYC admissions conversations included an unprompted ask for after-school, 13 percent of all conversations, with no marketing and no product to point to. Most recently, a family said they would continue with Alpha by trying it that way if after-school existed. The same pattern held in Nancy's prior private-school sales role, where the after-school question came up at least biweekly across a national territory. The demand also runs inside our own walls: Alpha parents nationwide ask what happens after the school day ends at 3:30, and today the answer is nothing.

Price acceptance is already proven at this exact number: Alpha Summer in the Hamptons has 174 paid registrations at $4,500 per week, with one price objection total. Alpha Hours asks the same families the same number for seven weeks instead of one.

### What no competitor can produce

What 100 weekly minutes of Timeback produces is something no school or human teacher in America can give a parent: a precise mastery map of where the child actually is in every subject, the child's measured learning velocity, and a computed answer to the only question that matters, exactly how many weeks at full Alpha until this child is at grade level, or the next one. The traditional school has the child six hours a day for years and produces a report card guessing subjectively at all of this. Seven weeks with us produces the map and the timeline. We do not compete with Kumon. We compete with six hours of no information, and we win in seven weeks.

## 4. Pricing

Priced per session, on value not hours. The anchor is the result, not hours delivered.

| Tier | Per session (about 7 weeks) | Full year (all 5 sessions) |
|---|---|---|
| Standard markets | $3,500 | $17,500 |
| Premium markets | $4,500 | $22,500 |
| Current Alpha families (5 percent discount) | $4,275 premium / $3,325 standard | $21,375 premium / $16,625 standard |
| Continuation rate (past 5 sessions, application on file) | $6,500 premium / $5,000 standard | n/a |

The pilot prices every campus at the premium tier ($4,500), Chicago included, matching the national capacity model.

### Why these prices hold

- We sell a product and measurable academic outcomes, not hours. A session is four hours a week for seven weeks, about 28 hours. At $4,500 that is roughly $161 an hour, just under elite 1:1 tutors ($200 to $300), who cover one subject; we cover multiple, supervised, with the data to prove progress.
- A single session is four to five times the most expensive mainstream competitor for the same span. The full year is roughly a third (30 to 40 percent) of full Alpha tuition ($40,000 to $75,000), which frames full enrollment as a natural upgrade, not a leap.
- The price point is already market-tested: 174 paid Alpha Summer registrations at $4,500 per week, one price objection in 174 transactions. At $4,500 per seven-week session, Alpha Hours is the same number for seven times the duration.
- We quote the session and full-year numbers, not the hour. The per-hour figure is for objection handling only.

## 7. Downside economics and the floors

Contribution per enrolled student is about $20,625 a year ($4,500 per session less about $375 in variable cost, times five sessions). Minimum operating cost per campus is about $278K, so break-even is **14 enrolled students per campus**, about 4 per cohort, under 4 percent of full capacity at the largest sites. Enrollment closes before each session starts and guides are hired to sold seats, so every staffing step is taken against booked revenue, and site margin holds between 70 and 83 percent at every fill level above break-even.

Exposure is capped by design: one-session enrollment, part-time staff, no capex, and a building Alpha already pays for mean the maximum downside at any site is seven weeks of payroll.

Two mechanical floors sit under it:

- **Cohort floor.** A cohort that has not sold 10 seats two weeks before session start does not open, and its families roll into the other weekday track or a Saturday block.
- **Site floor.** A campus below break-even at week 3 of Session 2 closes at session end, staff released, room returned to the campus. Week 3 is the checkpoint because attendance and re-enrollment intent, the program's own leading indicators, are both visible by then.

### Year 1 and the ramp

Session 2 opens at full capacity across all four pilot campuses: every cohort is on sale and the pilot enrolls every family the three markets will buy. Launch capacity is 1,256 enrolled ($28.3M a year run rate) with Chicago at its current 94 seats; the Chicago expansion in January 2027 steps the pilot to 2,948 enrolled and $66.3M a year from Session 3. Year 1 is four sessions, Sessions 2 through 5, one at launch capacity and three at expanded, so the Year 1 ceiling is about **$45M in pilot revenue and roughly $38M net at full fill**. No fill forecast is offered, because fill is one of the things Session 2 exists to measure, and the floors above cap the downside at every level.

New York launches Fall 2027 with the proven playbook, and the national build scales toward roughly $140M revenue at 40 campuses, with the middle and high school block lifting every participating campus ceiling by 50 percent from Fall 2027. The pace of that build is set by the pilot's measured conversion, re-enrollment, and fill numbers, not by this projection.

## 8. Operations and compliance

### Real estate

The building is a sunk cost Alpha already carries, whether it owns the campus or leases it, with after-hours and weekend access either way. Late-afternoon and Saturday use adds only marginal cost: about $150 to $250 per weekday, about $400 per Saturday (custodial, HVAC and lighting, security). Because the host is an Alpha campus, the facility fee is largely intracompany.

### Licensing

The key question in every state is whether the program counts as childcare or as exempt instruction. Alpha Hours is designed and marketed as academic instruction, not childcare, to qualify for state exemptions:

- Structure and market it as academic instruction with a defined curriculum, not as after-school care.
- Avoid meals where they trigger licensing (Florida especially); snacks only.
- Sign a formal host-campus facility-use agreement.
- Get a written exemption determination per state before opening, rather than self-declaring. For the pilot: Illinois, Massachusetts, Connecticut.
- Every block is curriculum-driven instruction end to end: timed mastery-academics sessions on Timeback and a structured life skills workshop. Flag the Saturday format specifically in each state's written exemption request.

### Safety and insurance

- FBI background checks on every hire: fingerprint criminal check, national sex offender registry, state child-abuse registry, verified complete before day one.
- Minimum two adults on site at all times.
- Insurance per site: general liability, professional liability, workers' compensation, and sexual abuse and molestation coverage (excluded from most general liability policies, must be added).

## 9. Rollout plan

### Phase 1 (Session 2, Fall 2026): three-market pilot, run as a conversion experiment

Three markets, four campuses: **Chicago, Boston (two sites), Greenwich (Armonk)**. Every campus opens at full capacity, all four cohorts on sale, no cap and no small-group phase: the pilot enrolls every family these markets will buy. Chicago opens on its current 94-seat footprint, 376 enrolled at capacity, ahead of the campus expansion in Phase 2. The floors in Section 7 carry the downside, and quality is carried by the guide training program, the Lead Guide at every site, and guides hired to sold seats so no room opens understaffed.

**NYC is deliberately not in the pilot.** It is the network's most valuable admissions pipeline and its most visible market, and the program does not put either at risk before the model is proven. New York gets Alpha Hours in Fall 2027, with a proven playbook. Launching at Session 2 rather than Session 1 gives campuses a settled start to the school year and the runway for guide hiring, state exemption determinations, and insurance.

### Pilot success criteria and measurement plan

The pilot succeeds on two measured numbers, not revenue: **conversion to full Alpha at or above 8 percent, and session-over-session re-enrollment at or above 70 percent.** The experiment is instrumented before it opens, in five commitments:

1. **The funnel is defined before Session 2 starts.** Conversion is tracked per family through six stages: paid Alpha Hours enrollment, structured conversion conversation held, application submitted, admitted, full-Alpha enrollment signed, first tuition payment. Stage-level tracking is what says whether a weak result is a marketing problem, an admissions problem, or a product problem.
2. **The trigger is mechanical.** The thresholds that fire the conversion conversation and the application invitation, mastery gains and session completion rate, are published before Session 2 opens, so the measured rate reflects the product, not the persuasiveness of whichever Lead Guide ran the conversation that week.
3. **The number is lift, not a raw count.** Conversion is measured against a matched baseline of non-Alpha-Hours inquiries from the same markets and the same window, because some share of these families would have applied anyway, and the case rests on what Alpha Hours adds.
4. **Sample size is respected.** Session 2's 1,256 families are reported as directional, with the margin of error stated; the go decision runs on the full 2,948 base by Session 3. Conversion is reported by campus, by cohort, by session count, and by market, and a material spread between Chicago and the East Coast markets is treated as a market-mix finding that reshapes rollout sequencing, not noise to average away.
5. **The decision rule is pre-committed.** Below 6 percent, national rollout and New York pause while the funnel stages say where it broke. Six to 10 percent, the rollout proceeds at a measured regional pace. Above 10 percent, full national go. Session 3's leadership conversation is the number and what it triggers, not another debate.

### Phase 2 (Session 3, January 2027): the Chicago expansion

Chicago's campus expansion lands and Alpha Hours steps with it, from 94 seats to 517, lifting the enrolled ceiling from 376 to 2,068 and the pilot from 1,256 to 2,948. One session of Chicago operating data, a waitlist built against the 94-seat footprint, and a trained guide bench sized to sold seats carry the step. This is the national build in miniature: the same room, the same playbook, five and a half times the seats, executed mid-pilot.

### Phase 3 (Session 4, Spring 2027): national launch

The playbook rolls across the country with two full sessions of operating data and the first measured conversion numbers. All campuses join here, with the exemption playbook in hand.

### Phase 4 (Fall 2027): New York, and the middle and high school block

Alpha Hours opens at Alpha New York with a year of operating data and a proven playbook, entering the network's most valuable market at full strength, at 125 seats and 500 enrolled at capacity. The same season, the 6:15 to 8:15 pm block launches, academics on Timeback with Alpha Max and Alpha X programming filling the workshop time, lifting every participating campus ceiling by 50 percent.

### Why these campuses

- **Chicago** is the network's largest campus: 94 seats at launch, 517 from January 2027, 2,068 enrolled at capacity. The pilot tests the model at the exact scale the national build depends on, and its mid-pilot expansion is a live rehearsal of scaling a site, in a market outside the East Coast corridor, which makes the measured conversion number generalize.
- **Greenwich and Boston** are dense UHNW markets at premium tuition where every conversion is worth the most, close enough that one launch team and a shared guide pool cover three campuses.
- **NYC is excluded on purpose:** the admissions pipeline there is already strong and the brand risk is highest, so New York opens Fall 2027, after the model is proven.

## 10. Risks and how we manage them

| Risk | Response |
|---|---|
| Reputation: Alpha Hours is many families' first Alpha experience and carries the school's brand | NYC is held out of the pilot entirely and opens Fall 2027 with a proven playbook. Quality is carried by the guide training program, guides staffed to sold seats, the Lead Guide owning quality at every site, and the AlphaTest Showcase putting proof on a stage every seven weeks. |
| A wrong-fit student disrupts the room for the families who paid | The week-1 exit process: every incident that pulls a guide off the room is logged the moment it happens, and two logged incidents in week 1 is an exit before week 2, with the incident record and a prorated refund. A child who requires a level of individual support that compromises the program for the rest of the room is the second trigger. Both standards are published at enrollment; the log makes the decision, the Head delivers it. |
| Students who need 1:1 support | The staffing model is 1 guide per 10 students and cannot deliver one-to-one support. This is publicly listed on the landing page before any family pays, which protects the ratio and filters at the top of the funnel instead of in week 1. |
| The arrival window for non-Alpha students | Outside schools dismiss around 3:00 and drop-off closes at 4:00. For most target families this is routine, and for the rest the two Saturday blocks carry the load. The window is held at 15 minutes on purpose: the block starts on time for everyone, every time. |
| Saturday competes with youth sports | Two Saturday blocks, two weekday tracks, and session-at-a-time enrollment let families rotate around sports seasons instead of choosing against them permanently. |
| Families park in Alpha Hours instead of converting | The structured conversion conversation, the mastery map, and the weeks-to-grade-level projection make the ceiling explicit. Any family past five sessions holds an Alpha application and pays the continuation rate ($6,500 premium, $5,000 standard), so a long-stayer is either pipeline by definition or premium revenue, and where a waitlist stands, non-applicants release the seat first. The Head is reviewed on conversion rate and full-time enrollments, not fill rate. |
| Guide supply at national scale | The candidate pool, public school teachers and retired teachers, exists at scale in every metro. The Alpha Hours training program (shadowing at Alpha NYC plus AI-first asynchronous training) standardizes quality before any guide runs a room. |
| A state treats the program as licensed childcare | Design each block as curriculum-driven instruction, avoid meals, sign a formal host-campus agreement, get a written exemption per state before opening, flagging the Saturday format specifically. |
| Low conversion to full Alpha | Even at zero conversion the program is profitable. The pre-committed decision rule caps the cost of a weak number: below 6 percent, the national rollout pauses while the six-stage funnel says where it broke. |
| Alpha's academic claims are its own internal data, not audited | Report each child's actual Timeback mastery data to that child's parents rather than leaning on network-wide claims. We do not promise 2x learning in one session; we promise the roadmap to 2x learning at full Alpha. |

Alpha Hours turns empty Alpha buildings into a profitable, premium after-school program that also functions as the lowest-cost, highest-intent enrollment channel Alpha has, because the families who try the model one session at a time are the ones most likely to pay full tuition for it.
