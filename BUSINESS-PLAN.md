## 1. What Alpha Hour Is

Alpha Hour is a paid, premium after-school program that runs inside Alpha School buildings after the regular school day ends. Families who do not attend Alpha get the accelerated Alpha experience in one hour: their child works Math and Reading/Writing on Timeback, Alpha's AI mastery platform (formerly branded 2 Hour Learning), supported in person by guides hired and trained specifically for this program.

The program does three jobs at once:

1. **It makes money.** Families pay a premium price per session, above every after-school competitor in the market.
2. **It uses real estate we already pay for.** Alpha campuses sit empty after the school day. Alpha Hour turns the evening into revenue at close to zero added facility cost.
3. **It feeds Alpha.** Alpha Hour is the lowest-friction way for a family to try the Alpha model without leaving their current school. A share of those families enroll full-time, and full Alpha tuition runs $40,000 to $75,000 per year.

Start market: grades K-8, at established Alpha campuses, scaling nationwide across the Alpha network.

## 2. The Program Model

### The accelerated Alpha experience, in one hour

A normal Alpha day gives students about two hours of core academics on Timeback. Alpha Hour distills that into a single focused hour, two evenings a week. Each hour the student works both subjects on the platform, roughly half on Math and half on Reading/Writing, adjusting to wherever they need the time. The software delivers the instruction and adapts to each child, so a child moves at their own level regardless of grade. The guide manages focus, effort, and motivation. That is the whole model: one hour, both subjects, mastery-based, two evenings a week.

**Both subjects are mandatory.** There is no single-subject option. Alpha Hour is one product, the accelerated Alpha hour, not a menu.

### Schedule and tracks

The host campus building is available to Alpha Hour after 4:45 pm, Monday through Thursday. A student does not come all four nights. Each family picks a **two-night track, Monday and Wednesday or Tuesday and Thursday**, and a time block within the evening. That keeps the commitment realistic for families while the building runs full every night. Two one-hour time blocks run back to back each evening:

| | Time |
|---|---|
| Setup | 4:45 to 5:00 pm (guides prepare, devices ready) |
| Block 1 | 5:00 to 6:00 pm |
| Transition | 6:00 to 6:15 pm (staggered pickup and drop-off) |
| Block 2 | 6:15 to 7:15 pm |

Both blocks are mixed K-8. Because the platform individualizes every child's work, grade level does not need to sort the room, which is exactly how Alpha's own classrooms run. Put together, every campus runs **four cohorts a week**: Monday/Wednesday early, Monday/Wednesday late, Tuesday/Thursday early, and Tuesday/Thursday late. The same room and the same guide team serve all four.

### Capacity is set per campus, and the goal is to fill every cohort

Each campus has a room capacity, the number of students it seats at once. Because two time blocks run each night, and each seat serves a Monday/Wednesday cohort and a separate Tuesday/Thursday cohort across the week, **enrolled students per campus is four times the room capacity**. A 100-seat room fills to 400 enrolled students: 100 per cohort, four cohorts. Filling every cohort to capacity is the operating goal. Pilot campus capacities are set out in Section 6.

### Enrollment: one session at a time

The academic year is divided into **five sessions**, each about seven weeks long (roughly 35 weeks of programming total). Families sign up **one session at a time**, with a **one-session minimum**, choosing a two-night track each time. They re-enroll session to session, and a family that stays all year completes all five.

Signing up one session at a time is deliberate. It lowers the barrier to trying Alpha Hour, which widens the top of the funnel, and it means far more distinct families cycle through each seat over a year than an annual-only plan would allow. Every one of those families is a full-time Alpha prospect who has lived the model.

### Why this design works

- **Throughput.** Two time blocks and two weekly tracks let one room serve four cohorts, so a campus enrolls four times its seat count without four times the staff or facility cost, because only one cohort is in the room at a time. This is the core of the money machine.
- **Real estate.** The space is a sunk cost Alpha already carries, whether it owns the campus or leases it, and it has after-hours access either way. Evening use adds only marginal cost (utilities, cleaning, coverage).
- **Guides do the right job.** Guides are not subject teachers. The platform teaches. Guides manage focus, effort, and motivation, which is what Alpha's own model relies on, so the training is short and the role is repeatable across sites.

## 3. Market Analysis

### The market is large, growing on the paid-tutoring side, and supply-constrained on the after-school side

- US tutoring and supplemental education is roughly a **$18.9 billion** industry (IBISWorld, 2025). The US private tutoring market is projected to grow at about **11% per year** through 2029 (Technavio, Grand View Research). The K-5 grade band is the fastest-growing segment.
- US after-school programs are a **$21.0 billion** industry (IBISWorld, 2025). Demand exceeds supply: about 7 million children are in structured after-school programs, and about **22.6 million more** would enroll if their families could get a spot (Afterschool Alliance, America After 3PM, 2025). The industry is shrinking on capacity, not demand.
- Roughly **36 to 37 million** US children are in grades K-8 (NCES). Tutoring participation rose from 19% to 24% of students between 2024 and 2025.
- Parents report willingness to pay about **$357 per month** (roughly $4,300 per year) to tutor one child (EdChoice, 2025), which is *above* what most incumbents charge. High-income families already spend up to about $9,000 per year on tutoring, test prep, and enrichment.

### Where competitors price, and the ceiling Alpha Hour clears

**No multi-subject after-school center charges above roughly $500 per month today.** Over a seven-week session that is about $875 for a single subject. Alpha Hour's two-subject session price sits well above that, in the same premium band as a Fusion Academy per-course term, which is the correct reference class.

### The opportunity: TAM, SAM, and why there is no ceiling

Alpha Hour is priced at $16,000 a year, so the market is the families who can pay for premium academics, and it is enormous relative to anything the current campus network can seat.

| Layer | Definition | Size |
|---|---|---|
| **TAM** | US K-8 students in households that can afford premium academics (about 4.4M students, roughly the top 12% of households by income) at $16,000 per year | **~$70B / year** |
| **SAM** | The share of those families in the roughly 30 metros where Alpha operates or is opening campuses (about 1.75M students) | **~$28B / year** |
| **SOM (today)** | What the current 40-campus network can physically seat at full capacity (10,920 students, see Section 7A) | **~$175M / year** |

The point is the ratio. Today's entire network at full capacity is about **0.6% of the serviceable market and roughly 0.25% of the total market**. Alpha Hour is not constrained by demand. It is constrained by how many campuses and seats exist, and Alpha has been adding roughly 20 campuses a year. There is no ceiling. As Alpha spreads across the country, Alpha Hour scales with it, toward a market measured in tens of billions.

Assumptions are conservative and can be tightened or loosened: a stricter income filter ($250k-plus households) still leaves a TAM in the tens of billions; a looser one (the full $40B the US already spends on K-8 tutoring and after-school) puts the total market higher.

### The whitespace Alpha Hour occupies

1. **Multi-subject rigor at a premium price.** Kumon and Mathnasium are single-subject worksheet or group programs. Nothing center-based offers personalized, mastery-based Math and Reading/Writing together above the $500 per month line.
2. **AI acceleration in a supervised, physical setting.** AI tutoring today is either a $4 to $45 per month unsupervised app (Khanmigo, Synthesis) or buried inside a $40,000+ full-time school (Alpha). No one sells the AI-adaptive model as a supervised after-school program.
3. **Outcomes, not seat time.** Legacy centers sell hours. Alpha Hour sells measurable progress, shown to parents continuously through Timeback mastery data.
4. **Demand overhang.** With 22.6 million children shut out of after-school programs and willingness to pay above incumbent prices, a premium entrant faces unmet demand, not a share fight.

## 4. Pricing

Alpha Hour is priced above every mainstream after-school competitor and positioned as a fraction of full Alpha tuition. Pricing is set on market value, not on the cost of the software. The anchor is the result (the accelerated Alpha hour and measurable grade-level progress), not hours delivered.

Families pay per session. Both subjects are always included.

| Tier | Per session (about 7 weeks) | Full year (all 5 sessions) |
|---|---|---|
| Standard markets (Alpha tuition $40k to $50k) | $2,500 | $12,500 |
| Premium markets (Alpha tuition $60k to $75k) | $3,200 | $16,000 |

- **Billing:** per session, with a one-session minimum. Families re-enroll session to session.
- **Premium markets** include New York, San Francisco, Palo Alto, Boston, Greenwich, and similar campuses where Alpha tuition is $60,000 or more.

### Why these prices hold

- A single session at $2,500 is more than three times the priciest mainstream competitor's cost for the same seven-week span, and it covers both subjects, not one. No parent comparison-shops it against Mathnasium. It sits just under a Fusion Academy per-course term, which is the right neighborhood.
- The full-year figure ($12,500 standard, $16,000 premium) is roughly 25 to 30 percent of full Alpha tuition. That frames full enrollment as a natural upgrade, not a leap.
- **We sell a place, not hours.** A session is two evenings a week for seven weeks, about 14 hours. At $3,200 that is roughly $228 an hour, which sits inside the range of elite 1:1 tutoring ($200 to $300), for a supervised, AI-personalized program covering both subjects at once. The point is that the program is priced against the outcome and against full Alpha tuition, not against a clock. Quote the session and full-year numbers in marketing; the per-hour figure is for objection handling only.

The platform teaches, so ratios can be leaner than active-instruction tutoring but should still read as premium. Guides scale with the block, not the site. A 100-student block needs about 10 guides plus a lead; a 40-student block needs about 4 plus a lead. Because the two blocks run back to back, the same team covers both.

## 6. Unit Economics and the East Coast Pilot P&L

### How the numbers scale

Costs fall into three groups:

- **Per student:** Timeback platform (about $150 per student per session, near the marginal cost of adding a seat to software Alpha already owns), supplies and rewards (about $30 per session), payment processing (3% of revenue), and local marketing (about $300 per student per year).
- **Per block (staffing):** guides scale with room capacity at about 1 per 10 students plus a lead. Guides work a 2.75-hour evening, four nights, across five seven-week sessions (35 program weeks). The same team serves all four cohorts, so guide cost per campus does not rise when a room's four cohorts fill. Loaded guide rates run about $40 per hour in Florida markets and $48 to $52 in the Northeast.
- **Per site (fixed):** host campus facility fee (about $35,000 a year, largely intracompany) and insurance (about $8,000).

Revenue per site = enrolled students x price per session x 5 sessions. Enrolled students = room capacity x 2 time blocks x 2 weekly tracks, which is four cohorts. Both subjects are always included.

Even at zero conversions, the downside is a highly profitable after-school business. That asymmetry is the core of the strategy.

## 8. Operations and Compliance

### Real estate

The host campus building is already paid for by daytime tuition, whether Alpha owns the campus or leases it. Either way Alpha controls the space and has after-hours access, so the evening use is a sunk cost, not a new one. Marginal evening costs are real and are budgeted in the site P&L: extended custodial hours, HVAC and lighting for the wing in use, and security or front-desk coverage. Because the program is one hour per block and the building is available from 4:45 pm, the evening footprint is short. Total incremental facility cost runs about $150 to $250 per evening, a fraction of leasing dedicated commercial space in the same neighborhoods. Because the host is an Alpha campus, the facility fee is largely intracompany.

### Licensing

The key question in every state is whether an evening program counts as childcare or as an exempt instructional program. Alpha Hour is designed to qualify as instruction:

- Structure and market it as academic instruction with a defined curriculum, not as after-school care.
- Do not serve meals in states where meals trigger licensing (Florida in particular); snacks only.
- Sign a formal written facility-use agreement with the host campus, which unlocks the school-site exemption in several states.
- Request a written exemption determination from each state agency before opening, rather than self-declaring. Rules vary: New York (OCFS single-purpose instruction exemption), Texas (HHSC single-skill exemption form), Florida (school-site and instructional exemptions), and others each have a specific path. The one-hour, single-purpose, curriculum-driven design strengthens the instruction argument in every state.

### Safety and insurance

- Background checks on every hire: fingerprint-based criminal check, national sex offender registry, and state child-abuse registry. Adopt each host campus's strictest standard as the floor. Budget $50 to $100 per hire.
- Two adults on site at all times.
- Insurance stack: general liability ($1M/$2M), professional liability, workers' compensation, and sexual abuse and molestation coverage (this is excluded from most general liability policies and must be added). Abuse/molestation minimum premiums start around $5,000 per year. The host campus will require being named as additional insured.

## 9. Rollout Plan

The five-session calendar sets the timing. Sessions run about seven weeks each across the school year, so Session 3 begins in January. That makes January 2027 the natural moment to open the national rollout on a fresh enrollment start.

### Why these campuses: VIP markets with proven demand

The pilot is not a random six. It is the East Coast VIP corridor: New York, Boston, Greenwich, Palm Beach, and Miami Beach. These campuses are chosen for four reasons, and the first is data.

1. **We already have demand data here.** Alpha runs its UHNW East Coast admissions events in exactly these five markets. The inquiry volume, event attendance, and interest lists from that program are direct evidence of concentrated demand for the Alpha model in each city. We are launching Alpha Hour where the funnel already shows families leaning in, not where we hope they will.
2. **These are the highest-value campuses in the network.** With the exception of Palm Beach, every pilot market carries full Alpha tuition of $60,000 to $75,000, the top of the entire network. Two things follow. Premium session pricing ($3,200) holds easily against that backdrop, and every family who converts to full Alpha is worth the most that a conversion can be worth anywhere: $300,000 or more in lifetime tuition. Starting the feeder in the highest-lifetime-value markets is the highest-return way to sequence the rollout.
3. **The wealth is dense and it talks.** New York, Greenwich and Armonk (the Fairfield and Westchester finance belt), Boston, and the South Florida markets of Palm Beach and Miami Beach hold some of the highest concentrations of ultra-high-net-worth families in the country, and South Florida is still absorbing the finance and family-wealth migration of the last several years. These families sit in tight social networks where a program spreads by word of mouth. Filling one block at Alpha New York seeds referrals that fill the next.
4. **Nancy's base and the corridor's density.** Nancy moves into Head of Alpha Hour from admissions leadership at Alpha New York, bringing existing family relationships and referral networks across the corridor. The Northeast campuses (New York, Greenwich and Armonk, both Boston sites) are close enough that one coordinator and a shared guide pool can cover several sites, which lowers the cost and risk of standing up the pilot.

In short, we start where we can prove demand, charge the most, and convert the most valuable families, then take the playbook national in January 2027.

**Phase 1: East Coast premium pilot (Fall 2026, Sessions 1 and 2).**
Launch across five premium East Coast markets, six campuses since Boston has two: Alpha New York, Boston (two sites), Greenwich (Armonk), Palm Beach, and Miami Beach. Nancy's base and existing East Coast relationships make this the fastest region to stand up. Capacities are set per campus (Section 6), and the goal is to fill every block. Use the fall to lock the operating playbook, guide training, licensing exemptions per state, the five-session calendar, and the parent conversion process. Prove the per-site economics and the conversion rate before scaling.

**Phase 2: National rollout (January 2027, Session 3 onward).**
Open the rest of the country starting with the January session. Expand to campuses across every established metro, including the Fall 2026 openings, moving toward roughly 18 sites through 2027. Add campus coordinators. Formalize the Alpha Hour to full-Alpha conversion handoff so every family gets an intentional enrollment conversation, since research shows feeder conversion only materializes when the school builds that handoff.

**Phase 3: Full network (2027 into 2028).**
Run Alpha Hour at essentially every Alpha campus, roughly 40 sites. Move to a regional coordinator structure. Evaluate extending the market beyond K-8.

## 10. Risks and How We Manage Them

| Risk | Response |
|---|---|
| Part-time evening guide roles are hard to fill and turn over | Blend the role with Alpha campus afternoon shifts to create real full-time jobs. |
| A state treats the program as licensed childcare | Design as one-hour academic instruction, avoid meals, sign a formal host-campus agreement, get a written exemption per state before opening. |
| Session sign-up creates churn between sessions | Churn widens the feeder funnel; manage re-enrollment with visible progress reports at the end of each session, and keep seats full with new trial families. |
| Price resistance | Lead with the session outcome and the "one third of Alpha" frame; hold the per-hour math for objections; the mainstream market is far below this price, so we are not compared against Kumon. |
| Low conversion to full Alpha | Even at zero conversion the program is profitable; build an intentional conversion handoff to hit the 8 to 12% base case. |
| Alpha's academic claims are its own, not independently audited | Report each child's actual Timeback and MAP data to that child's parents rather than leaning on network-wide marketing claims. |
