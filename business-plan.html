Alpha Hours

Business Plan

Prepared by Nancy Wisniewski Torvund, proposed Head of Alpha Hours  ·  July 23, 2026  ·  Draft for review

1What Alpha Hours is

Alpha Hours is a paid, premium after-school and Saturday program that runs inside Alpha School buildings after the regular school day ends. It delivers the Alpha day, mastery-based academics on Timeback followed by a life skills workshop, to two groups: families who do not yet attend Alpha, and current Alpha students whose school day ends at 3:30. The child works on Timeback, Alpha's AI mastery platform, supported in person by guides hired and trained specifically for this program.

The program does four jobs at once:

  1. It makes money. Families pay a premium price per session, above every after-school competitor in the market.
  2. It uses real estate we already pay for. Alpha campuses sit empty in the late afternoon and on weekends. Alpha Hours turns those hours into revenue at close to zero added facility cost.
  3. It feeds Alpha. Alpha Hours is the lowest-friction, paid way for a family to try the Alpha model without leaving their current school. A share of those families enroll full-time, and full Alpha tuition runs $40,000 to $75,000 per year. Conversion is the one modeled number in this plan, and Phase 1 exists to replace it with a measured one.
  4. It serves Alpha's own families. Almost every Alpha campus ends the school day at 3:30 with nothing behind it. Alpha Hours closes that gap on every campus, and gets paid a premium price to do it. Current Alpha students enroll at the same session price with a 5 percent family discount and transition directly on campus at the end of the school day.

Success is measured on all four: site profit, building utilization, conversions to full Alpha, and current-family enrollment. Underneath them sit Alpha's three commitments, each with its own measurement in Alpha Hours: kids love school (re-enrollment, attendance, student surveys), learn 2x (every student's progress and learning velocity in Timeback, session over session), and learn life skills (the AlphaTest Showcase that ends every session). Alpha's accelerated-learning figures are Alpha's own internal data.

Start market: grades K-8 at established Alpha campuses, scaling nationwide. A dedicated middle and high school evening block launches Fall 2027.

2The program model

The Alpha day, after school

An Alpha day runs primarily on two things: mastery-based academics on Timeback and life skills workshops. Alpha Hours compresses that whole day into one or two blocks. A weekday block is two hours: a ten-minute Limitless launch, two 25-minute academic sessions on Timeback with a short break between, a 40-minute life skills workshop, and ten minutes of shoutouts. Each of the two Saturday blocks runs the same day at double length: four 25-minute academic sessions, an 80-minute workshop, and a snack break in the middle. Either way the child gets the same week: 100 minutes of focused, adaptive mastery academics and 80 minutes of life skills. Timeback delivers the instruction and adapts to each child. The guide holds focus, effort, and motivation, and leads the workshop.

Motivation and incentives

The child has already been through six hours of regular school and walks in tired at 4 pm, so the block needs a built-in reason to give full effort. Alpha already has this reason, built into the model twice. First, Timeback's own promise carries into every block: get through your academics with focus, and the rest of the block is the life skills workshop, the part built around things kids actually love. The academics are the shortest path to the good part, exactly the trade Alpha kids make every day. Second, the extrinsic model: students earn the Alpha currency for XP earned in Timeback and can spend it in the Alpha Hours Emporium.

AlphaTest Showcase

Every session ends with an AlphaTest Showcase in the final week. Students are tested on the life skills they have worked on across the session's workshops, and parents come to see it. The Showcase is how the life skills commitment gets measured instead of assumed, and it puts every parent in the building in the same week they receive their child's mastery map and weeks-to-grade-level report, which makes it the natural setting for the re-enrollment and full-Alpha conversation.

Schedule and tracks

Alpha Hours runs Monday through Thursday afternoons and Saturday, inside existing Alpha campuses. The weekday block starts right where the Alpha school day ends, so the building never goes dark and every child is home for dinner. Each family picks a two-day weekday track, Monday and Wednesday or Tuesday and Thursday, or a single Saturday block, morning or afternoon.

Capacity

Room capacity is the seats filled at one time, set per campus. Enrolled students per campus is four times the room capacity: two weekday tracks plus two Saturday blocks. A 100-seat room fills to 400 enrolled students. From Fall 2027, the middle and high school block lifts the ceiling to six times room capacity. The operating goal is to fill every cohort to capacity.

Enrollment: one session at a time

The year runs five sessions, each about seven weeks (about 35 program weeks). Families sign up one session at a time, choosing a weekday track or a Saturday block, with a one-session minimum, and re-enroll session to session. Current Alpha students enroll on the same terms at the same session price with a 5 percent family discount.

Prospective families enroll at the standard rate for any five sessions, consecutive or not, essentially a full program year. Continuing past five sessions requires a completed Alpha application on file and the continuation rate: $6,500 per session in premium markets, $5,000 standard. No family is exited for staying: the seat reprices and the family enters the admissions funnel by definition. Where a cohort holds a waitlist, families with applications on file hold their seats and families without release them first. Current Alpha students are exempt.

3Market analysis

The market

US families already spend about $40 billion a year on tutoring and after-school combined. Tutoring is an $18.9 billion industry growing about 11 percent annually, with K-5 the fastest segment (IBISWorld, 2025); tutoring participation rose from 19 to 24 percent of students between 2024 and 2025. After-school is a $21.0 billion industry, with 7 million children enrolled and 22.6 million more whose families want a spot and cannot get one (Afterschool Alliance, 2025). High-income families already spend up to about $9,000 per child per year on tutoring and enrichment.

Demand evidence, bottom-up

After-school is unprompted inbound demand, not only a hypothesis. In the last 14 days, 3 of 23 NYC admissions conversations included an unprompted ask for after-school, 13 percent of all conversations, with no marketing and no product to point to. The same pattern held in Nancy's prior private-school sales role, where the after-school question came up at least biweekly across a national territory. The demand also runs inside our own walls: Alpha parents nationwide ask what happens after the school day ends at 3:30, and today the answer is nothing. Price acceptance is already proven at this exact number: Alpha Summer in the Hamptons has 174 paid registrations at $4,500 per week, with one price objection total.

What no competitor can produce

100 weekly minutes of Timeback produces something no school or human teacher in America can give a parent: a precise mastery map of where the child actually is in every subject, the child's measured learning velocity, and a computed answer to exactly how many weeks at full Alpha until this child is at grade level, or the next one. The traditional school has the child six hours a day for years and produces a report card guessing subjectively at all of this. Seven weeks with us produces the map and the timeline. We do not compete with Kumon. We compete with six hours of no information, and we win in seven weeks.

6Unit economics and the pilot P&L

How the numbers scale

  • Revenue = enrolled × price per session × 5 sessions. (Enrolled = room capacity × 4.)
  • Per student per year: Timeback $150 × 5 sessions, supplies and rewards $30 × 5, payment processing 3 percent, local marketing $300. About $1,875 per student per year.
  • Guides: 1 per 10 concurrent seats, part-time at $50,000. The same guide serves all four cohorts, so one guide supports up to 40 enrolled students, about $1,250 of guide cost per enrolled student at even fill.
  • Per-campus fixed cost: Lead Guide $75,000, Campus Coordinator $50,000 (50 percent role), facility about $45,000 a year, insurance about $8,000.

Margin runs about 83 percent at capacity, higher at larger campuses where the fixed team spreads over more revenue.

The feeder value, which is larger than the program itself

  • One family that converts from Alpha Hours to full Alpha is worth $160,000 to $325,000 in lifetime tuition.
  • Every session ends with a conversion artifact: the child's mastery map and a personalized projection of weeks-to-grade-level at full Alpha, so the parent leaves holding a number, not an impression.
  • Private schools typically convert 5 to 8 percent of inquiries, and an inquiry is a stranger with a phone number, while every Alpha Hours family has paid $4,500 and watched their own child's mastery data climb for seven weeks. We model conversion at 8 to 12 percent, and Phase 1 exists to replace that assumption with our own measured number.
  • Feeder sensitivity at a $240,000 midpoint: on the pilot's 2,948 enrolled at expanded capacity, 4 percent conversion is about $28M, 8 percent about $57M, 12 percent about $85M. Even at half the modeled rate the feeder outearns the program's own profit.

Effective customer acquisition cost goes negative

A private school typically spends $3,700 to $5,800 in marketing to win one enrolled student. Alpha Hours flips that: the prospective family pays us $4,500 a session while they decide, so evaluating Alpha earns money instead of costing it. The effective acquisition cost per full enrollment is negative. Even at zero conversions, the downside is a highly profitable after-school business.

7Downside economics and the floors

Contribution per enrolled student is about $20,625 a year ($4,500 per session less about $375 in variable cost, times five sessions). Minimum operating cost per campus is about $278K, so break-even is 14 enrolled students per campus, about 4 per cohort, under 4 percent of full capacity at the largest sites. Enrollment closes before each session starts and guides are hired to sold seats, so every staffing step is taken against booked revenue, and site margin holds between 70 and 83 percent at every fill level above break-even. Exposure is capped by design: one-session enrollment, part-time staff, no capex, and a building Alpha already pays for mean the maximum downside at any site is seven weeks of payroll.

  • Cohort floor. A cohort that has not sold 10 seats two weeks before session start does not open, and its families roll into the other weekday track or a Saturday block.
  • Site floor. A campus below break-even at week 3 of Session 2 closes at session end, staff released, room returned to the campus. Week 3 is the checkpoint because attendance and re-enrollment intent are both visible by then.

8Operations and compliance

Real estate

The building is a sunk cost Alpha already carries, whether it owns the campus or leases it, with after-hours and weekend access either way. Late-afternoon and Saturday use adds only marginal cost: about $150 to $250 per weekday, about $400 per Saturday (custodial, HVAC and lighting, security). Because the host is an Alpha campus, the facility fee is largely intracompany.

Licensing

The key question in every state is whether the program counts as childcare or as exempt instruction. Alpha Hours is designed and marketed as academic instruction: a defined curriculum, no meals where they trigger licensing (Florida especially, snacks only), a formal host-campus facility-use agreement, and a written exemption determination per state before opening (for the pilot: Illinois, Massachusetts, Connecticut). Every block is curriculum-driven instruction end to end: timed mastery-academics sessions on Timeback and a structured life skills workshop. Flag the Saturday format specifically in each state's written exemption request.

Safety and insurance

  • FBI background checks on every hire: fingerprint criminal check, national sex offender registry, state child-abuse registry, verified complete before day one.
  • Minimum two adults on site at all times.
  • Insurance per site: general liability, professional liability, workers' compensation, and sexual abuse and molestation coverage (excluded from most general liability policies, must be added).

9Rollout plan

Phase 1 (Session 2, Fall 2026): three-market pilot, run as a conversion experiment

Three markets, four campuses: Chicago, Boston (two sites), Greenwich (Armonk). Every campus opens at full capacity, all four cohorts on sale, no cap and no small-group phase: the pilot enrolls every family these markets will buy. Chicago opens on its current 94-seat footprint, 376 enrolled at capacity, ahead of the campus expansion in Phase 2. NYC is deliberately not in the pilot. It is the network's most valuable admissions pipeline and its most visible market, and the program does not put either at risk before the model is proven. New York gets Alpha Hours in Fall 2027, with a proven playbook. Launching at Session 2 rather than Session 1 gives campuses a settled start to the school year and the runway for guide hiring, state exemption determinations, and insurance.

Pilot success criteria and measurement plan

The pilot succeeds on two measured numbers, not revenue: conversion to full Alpha at or above 8 percent, and session-over-session re-enrollment at or above 70 percent. The experiment is instrumented before it opens, in five commitments:

  1. The funnel is defined before Session 2 starts. Conversion is tracked per family through six stages: paid Alpha Hours enrollment, structured conversion conversation held, application submitted, admitted, full-Alpha enrollment signed, first tuition payment.
  2. The trigger is mechanical. The thresholds that fire the conversion conversation and the application invitation, mastery gains and session completion rate, are published before Session 2 opens.
  3. The number is lift, not a raw count. Conversion is measured against a matched baseline of non-Alpha-Hours inquiries from the same markets and the same window.
  4. Sample size is respected. Session 2's 1,256 families are reported as directional, with the margin of error stated; the go decision runs on the full 2,948 base by Session 3. Conversion is reported by campus, by cohort, by session count, and by market.
  5. The decision rule is pre-committed. Below 6 percent, national rollout and New York pause while the funnel stages say where it broke. Six to 10 percent, the rollout proceeds at a measured regional pace. Above 10 percent, full national go.

Phase 2 (Session 3, January 2027): the Chicago expansion

Chicago's campus expansion lands and Alpha Hours steps with it, from 94 seats to 517, lifting the enrolled ceiling from 376 to 2,068 and the pilot from 1,256 to 2,948. One session of Chicago operating data, a waitlist built against the 94-seat footprint, and a trained guide bench sized to sold seats carry the step. This is the national build in miniature: the same room, the same playbook, five and a half times the seats, executed mid-pilot.

Phase 3 (Session 4, Spring 2027): national launch

The playbook rolls across the country with two full sessions of operating data and the first measured conversion numbers. All campuses join here, with the exemption playbook in hand.

Phase 4 (Fall 2027): New York, and the middle and high school block

Alpha Hours opens at Alpha New York with a year of operating data and a proven playbook, entering the network's most valuable market at full strength, at 125 seats and 500 enrolled at capacity. The same season, the 6:15 to 8:15 pm block launches, academics on Timeback with Alpha Max and Alpha X programming filling the workshop time, lifting every participating campus ceiling by 50 percent.

Why these campuses

  • Chicago is the network's largest campus: 94 seats at launch, 517 from January 2027, 2,068 enrolled at capacity. The pilot tests the model at the exact scale the national build depends on, and its mid-pilot expansion is a live rehearsal of scaling a site, in a market outside the East Coast corridor, which makes the measured conversion number generalize.
  • Greenwich and Boston are dense UHNW markets at premium tuition where every conversion is worth the most, close enough that one launch team and a shared guide pool cover three campuses.
  • NYC is excluded on purpose: the admissions pipeline there is already strong and the brand risk is highest, so New York opens Fall 2027, after the model is proven.
