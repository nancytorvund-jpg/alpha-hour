1What Alpha Hours Is

Alpha Hours is a paid, premium after-school and Saturday program that runs inside Alpha School buildings after the regular school day ends and on weekends. Families who do not attend Alpha get the complete Alpha day in miniature: their child works Math and Reading/Writing on Timeback, Alpha's AI mastery platform (formerly branded 2 Hour Learning), and takes part in a life skills workshop, supported in person by guides hired and trained specifically for this program.

The program does three jobs at once:

  1. It makes money. Families pay a premium price per session, above every after-school competitor in the market.
  2. It uses real estate we already pay for. Alpha campuses sit empty after the school day and on weekends. Alpha Hours turns those hours into revenue at close to zero added facility cost.
  3. It feeds Alpha. Alpha Hours is the lowest-friction way for a family to try the Alpha model without leaving their current school. A share of those families enroll full-time, and full Alpha tuition runs $40,000 to $75,000 per year.

Start market: grades K-8, at established Alpha campuses, scaling nationwide across the Alpha network.

2The Program Model

The complete Alpha day, after school

An Alpha day runs on two things: mastery academics on Timeback and life skills workshops. Alpha Hours compresses that whole day into each sitting. A weekday sitting is two hours: a ten-minute Limitless launch, two 25-minute academic sessions on Timeback (one Math, one Reading/Writing) with a short break between, a 40-minute life skills workshop, and ten minutes of shoutouts. The Saturday block runs the same day at double length: four 25-minute academic sessions, an 80-minute workshop, and a snack break in the middle. Either way the child gets the same week: 100 minutes of focused, adaptive mastery academics and 80 minutes of life skills. The software delivers the instruction and adapts to each child. The guide holds focus, effort, and motivation, and leads the workshop. That is the whole model: the complete Alpha day, academics and life skills, in one sitting.

Both subjects are mandatory. There is no single-subject option. Alpha Hours is one product, the complete Alpha day, not a menu.

Schedule and tracks

The host campus building is available to Alpha Hours after 4:45 pm Monday through Thursday, and on Saturdays. A student does not come every day. Each family picks a two-night weekday track, Monday and Wednesday or Tuesday and Thursday, or a Saturday block, morning or afternoon. That keeps the commitment realistic while the building runs full every session.

Every campus runs four cohorts a week: Monday/Wednesday, Tuesday/Thursday, Saturday morning, and Saturday afternoon. The same room and the same guide team serve all four, and every cohort gets the same four weekly hours.

Capacity is set per campus, and the goal is to fill every cohort

Each campus has a room capacity, the seats it fills at one time. Enrolled students per campus is four times the room capacity: two weekday tracks plus two Saturday blocks. A 100-seat room fills to 400 enrolled students. Filling every cohort to capacity is the operating goal.

Enrollment: one session at a time

The academic year is divided into five sessions, each about seven weeks long (roughly 35 weeks of programming total). Families sign up one session at a time, with a one-session minimum. They re-enroll session to session, and a family that stays all year completes all five.

Signing up one session at a time is deliberate. It lowers the barrier to trying Alpha Hours, which widens the top of the funnel, and it means far more distinct families cycle through each seat over a year than an annual-only plan would allow. Every one of those families is a full-time Alpha prospect who has lived the model.

Why this design works

  • Throughput. Two weekday tracks and two Saturday blocks let one room serve four cohorts, so a campus enrolls four times its seat count without four times the staff or facility cost, because only one cohort is in the room at a time. This is the core of the money machine.
  • Real estate. The space is a sunk cost Alpha already carries, whether it owns the campus or leases it, and it has after-hours and weekend access either way. Evening and Saturday use adds only marginal cost (utilities, cleaning, coverage).
  • Guides do the right job. The platform teaches the academics. Guides do not teach academics; they hold focus, effort, and motivation, and lead the life skills workshop and the launch and shoutout rituals, which is what Alpha's own model relies on, so the training is short and the role is repeatable across sites.

3Market Analysis

The market is large, growing on the paid-tutoring side, and supply-constrained on the after-school side

  • US tutoring and supplemental education is roughly a $18.9 billion industry (IBISWorld, 2025). The US private tutoring market is projected to grow at about 11% per year through 2029. The K-5 grade band is the fastest-growing segment.
  • US after-school programs are a $21.0 billion industry (IBISWorld, 2025). About 7 million children are in structured after-school programs, and about 22.6 million more would enroll if their families could get a spot (Afterschool Alliance, 2025). The industry is shrinking on capacity, not demand.
  • Roughly 36 to 37 million US children are in grades K-8 (NCES). Tutoring participation rose from 19% to 24% of students between 2024 and 2025.
  • Parents report willingness to pay about $357 per month (roughly $4,300 per year) to tutor one child (EdChoice, 2025), which is above what most incumbents charge. High-income families already spend up to about $9,000 per year on tutoring, test prep, and enrichment.

The whitespace Alpha Hours occupies

  1. Multi-subject rigor at a premium price. Nothing center-based offers personalized, mastery-based Math and Reading/Writing together above the $500 per month line.
  2. AI acceleration in a supervised, physical setting. AI tutoring today is either a $4 to $45 per month unsupervised app or buried inside a $40,000+ full-time school. No one sells the AI-adaptive model as a supervised after-school program.
  3. Outcomes, not seat time. Legacy centers sell hours. Alpha Hours sells measurable progress, shown to parents continuously through Timeback mastery data.
  4. Demand overhang. With 22.6 million children shut out of after-school programs and willingness to pay above incumbent prices, a premium entrant faces unmet demand, not a share fight.

4Pricing

Alpha Hours is priced above every mainstream after-school competitor and positioned as a fraction of full Alpha tuition. Pricing is set on market value, not on the cost of the software. The anchor is the result, not hours delivered. Families pay per session. Both subjects are always included.

Billing is per session, with a one-session minimum. Families re-enroll session to session. The East Coast pilot prices every campus at the premium tier; the standard tier is a flex option for lower-tuition national metros.

  • A single session at $4,500 is four to five times the priciest mainstream competitor's cost for the same seven-week span, and it covers both subjects, not one. It sits just under a Fusion Academy per-course term, which is the right neighborhood.
  • The full-year figure ($22,500 premium) is roughly a third (30 to 40 percent) of full Alpha tuition. That frames full enrollment as a natural upgrade, not a leap.
  • We sell a place, not hours. A session is four hours a week for seven weeks, about 28 hours. At $4,500 that is roughly $161 an hour, just under elite 1:1 tutors ($200 to $300), who cover one subject; we cover two, supervised, with the data to prove progress. The program is priced against the outcome and against full Alpha tuition, not against a clock. Quote the session and full-year numbers in marketing; the per-hour figure is for objection handling only.

Staffing ratios

The platform teaches the academics, so ratios can be leaner than active-instruction tutoring but should still read as premium. Guides do not teach academics; they hold focus, effort, and motivation, and lead the life skills workshop and the launch and shoutout rituals. Because blocks are mixed K-8, staff each block at about 1 guide per 10 students, with extra attention to the youngest, and a two-adult minimum whenever children are present. Younger, K-2-heavy sites should staff closer to 1 per 8. Guides scale with the block, not the site: a 100-student block needs about 10 guides plus a lead; a 40-student block needs about 4 plus a lead. The same team covers both blocks.

6Unit Economics and the East Coast Pilot P&L

How the numbers scale

  • Per student: Timeback platform (~$150 per student per session, near the marginal cost of adding a seat to software Alpha already owns), supplies and rewards (~$30 per session), payment processing (3% of revenue), local marketing (~$300 per student per year).
  • Per block (staffing): guides scale with room capacity at about 1 per 10 plus a lead, working about 20 staff-hours a week (2.5-hour evenings four times a week plus a roughly 10-hour Saturday), across five seven-week sessions. The same team serves all four cohorts, so guide cost per campus does not rise as the cohorts fill. Loaded guide rates run about $40 per hour in Florida markets and $48 to $52 in the Northeast.
  • Per site (fixed): host campus facility fee (~$45,000 a year, largely intracompany) and insurance (~$8,000).

Revenue per site = enrolled students × price per session × 5 sessions. Enrolled students per campus is four times the room capacity: two weekday tracks plus two Saturday blocks. A 100-seat room fills to 400 enrolled students. Both subjects always included.

The feeder value, which is larger than the program itself

  • One family that converts from Alpha Hours to full Alpha is worth $160,000 to $325,000 in lifetime tuition (4 to 6 years at $40,000 to $65,000).
  • Alpha Hours is the strongest kind of trial that exists: the family pays, the child attends every week for the session, and the parent watches mastery data the whole time.
  • Taking the pilot's 1,720 enrolled families and a conservative 8 to 12% conversion, the pilot produces roughly 140 to 205 full-time enrollments. Because these are the network's highest-tuition markets, the pilot pipeline is worth $40 million to $60 million in future tuition, on top of the program's own profit.

Effective customer acquisition cost goes negative

A private school normally spends $3,700 to $5,800 in marketing per enrolled student. With Alpha Hours, the prospect pays Alpha $4,500 per session during the consideration period, and the program is profitable. The effective acquisition cost per full enrollment is negative. The channel pays you. Even at zero conversions, the downside is a highly profitable after-school business.

8Operations and Compliance

Real estate

The host campus building is already paid for by daytime tuition, whether Alpha owns the campus or leases it. Either way Alpha controls the space and has after-hours and weekend access, so the evening and Saturday use is a sunk cost, not a new one. Marginal costs (extended custodial hours, HVAC and lighting for the wing in use, security or front-desk coverage) run about $150 to $250 per weekday evening, about $400 per Saturday, a fraction of leasing commercial space. Because the host is an Alpha campus, the facility fee is largely intracompany.

Licensing

The key question in every state is whether an evening or Saturday program counts as childcare or as exempt instruction. Alpha Hours is designed to qualify as instruction: structure and market it as academic instruction with a defined curriculum; avoid meals in states where they trigger licensing (Florida especially); sign a formal facility-use agreement with the host campus; and request a written exemption determination from each state agency before opening. Every block is curriculum-driven instruction end to end: timed mastery-academics sessions on Timeback and a structured life skills workshop. Flag the Saturday format specifically in each state's written exemption request.

Safety and insurance

  • Background checks on every hire (fingerprint criminal check, national sex offender registry, state child-abuse registry); adopt each host campus's strictest standard.
  • Two adults on site at all times.
  • Insurance: general liability ($1M/$2M), professional liability, workers' compensation, and sexual abuse and molestation coverage (excluded from most general liability policies, must be added). The host campus will require being named as additional insured.

9Rollout Plan

The five-session calendar sets the timing. Session 3 begins in January, so January 2027 is the natural moment to open the national rollout on a fresh enrollment start.

Why these campuses: VIP markets with proven demand

  1. We already have demand data here. Alpha runs its UHNW East Coast admissions events in exactly these five markets. The inquiry and interest data are direct evidence of demand. We launch where the funnel already shows families leaning in.
  2. These are the highest-value campuses. Every pilot market except Palm Beach carries full Alpha tuition of $60,000 to $75,000. Premium pricing holds, and every conversion is worth $300,000 or more in lifetime tuition. Start the feeder where lifetime value is highest.
  3. The wealth is dense and it talks. These metros hold some of the highest concentrations of UHNW families in the country, in tight social networks where a program spreads by word of mouth.
  4. Nancy's base and the corridor's density. Nancy moves into Head of Alpha Hours from admissions leadership at Alpha New York, bringing relationships across the corridor, and the Northeast campuses are close enough that one coordinator and a shared guide pool can cover several sites.

Phase 1: East Coast premium pilot (Fall 2026). Launch across five premium East Coast markets, six campuses since Boston has two: Alpha New York, Boston (two sites), Greenwich (Armonk), Palm Beach, and Miami Beach. Lock the operating playbook, guide training, licensing exemptions, the five-session calendar, and the conversion process.

Phase 2: National rollout (January 2027, Session 3 onward). Open the rest of the country at the start of the January session, moving toward roughly 18 sites through 2027. Add campus coordinators. Formalize the conversion handoff so every family gets an intentional enrollment conversation.

Phase 3: Full network (2027 into 2028). Run Alpha Hours at essentially every Alpha campus, roughly 40 sites and growing. Move to a regional coordinator structure. Evaluate extending the market beyond K-8.
